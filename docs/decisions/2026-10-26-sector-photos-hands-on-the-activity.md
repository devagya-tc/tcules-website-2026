Each sector card's photograph shows the main part of the activity its sector is about, cropped close,
with no faces: a pen on a chart, a parcel passing hands, keys dropped into a palm. Cover the label and
the photo alone names the sector. HealthTech shows people holding hands, with no older people and no
medical professionals or medicine. This replaces the unlogged 19 Oct 2026 direction, "the people a
sector serves, in a good moment".

**Why.** The owner's picks were "good because they don't show faces and focus on the main part". The
tcules.com marquee is the reference: Foodport shows food, Athleads an athlete, Phoenix a chart. Each one
is direct about its subject without being a stereotype. On HealthTech the owner said "we don't have to show
old people taking medicine or old people receiving care. that's the stereotype we want to avoid",
"I would actually like NO OLD person here", and that showing medical professionals was "still horrible".

**The mistake it prevents.** Three rejected rounds for the home band on 26 Oct 2026:
- **Metaphors:** a chisel rack for AI and developer tools, card-catalogue drawers for B2B SaaS, a pill
  organiser for HealthTech. The owner called them "not relevant to the industry at all".
- **Staged scenes of the client's customer's world:** a hard hat and a building for Dealpath, a site
  engineer for BuildTwin. These read as construction, not as the sector.
- **Faces and portraits:** smiling stock, plus older people being cared for, or clinicians, for
  HealthTech.

**How to apply.** `HOME_SECTOR_PHOTOS` in `src/lib/photos.ts`, and any later sector or industry
imagery. Brief a photo search in words first, agree it with the owner, then source it:
- client → product → what the sector is about
- one close action showing that, with hands and an object, and no face

Judge each pick with the cover-the-label test, not by whether the set matches in tone.
