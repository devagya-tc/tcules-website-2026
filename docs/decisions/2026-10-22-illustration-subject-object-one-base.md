Illustrations draw their subject as a recognisable object, not as an abstract diagram of planes and panels.
Every service hero is built on one shared base, and the object on it changes per service. The craft standard:
- a few large, simple masses;
- detail only at the one focal point;
- one hue plus the one accent;
- plenty of empty space.

An assembled object, with parts attached to one body, may be explored as an alternative to an exploded
stack. Process lines and conveyors are not used, and neither are labels as tabs built into objects.

**Why.** Devagya kept three ideas from the 22 Oct references: draw the subject as an object (R1, R2, R5), one base for every hero (R3, Integr), and the craft (R4, R5). Assembling (R5) is to be explored. Process lines (R6) and tab labels (R4) were rejected: "we don't want to show process, we want to show what's our unique selling point."

**The mistake it prevents.** The six shipped service heroes are abstract planes and panels, so a stranger can't
tell what any of them is about. They also share no common base, so the set never reads as one family. Four of the six fell back on two shapes: a stack with a thread, or a row of standing panels.

**How to apply.**
- Design the shared base once, for all six heroes, before any single hero.
- Each hero's own silhouette (gate 8) comes from the object on the base, not from a new construction.
- The stranger test (gate 7) should get the subject from the object as well as the change.
