**Corners.** Illustrations may use rounded corners, "since we're using rounded corners on the website as well". This replaces ch05 §4a's square-corners rule.

**Planes.** Planes stay flat by default. A plane or object is given thickness only when the thickness can be
justified, meaning it carries meaning rather than just looking solid. Jatin liked flat planes more than the
thick-plane experiments.

**Fills.** Tonal fills stay out. They "usually result in generic drawings". They may be tried in an experiment,
but they don't ship without a ruling.

**Type.** No type is baked into objects, because it won't read at small sizes and only adds noise. This
restates `2026-09-24-no-image-carries-type`; ch05 §4b's removable annotation layer is unchanged.

**Why.** These came from reviewing the 22 Oct references (`docs/research/illustration-references.md`) against
ch05 §4a. Rounded corners match the site. Thickness is what makes R1 and R4 feel tangible, but Jatin preferred
flat. Tonal fills are what make most isometric work look interchangeable.

**The mistake it prevents.** Copying the references' look wholesale: thick rounded trays with tonal fills and
type on them. That is the generic isometric look the companion ruling
(`2026-10-22-illustrations-show-the-usp`) rules out.

**How to apply.**
- When a concept uses thickness, it says in the concept what that thickness means, for example "a tray that holds".
- If it can't say, the plane stays flat.
