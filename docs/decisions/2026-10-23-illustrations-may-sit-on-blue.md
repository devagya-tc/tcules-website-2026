An illustration may be placed on a `brandeis-500` ground: "sitting on blue is fine", and the owner chose the blue island for the enquiry close's drawing over an ink island ("do #2 but draw on the blue island").

**Why.** The enquiry close's ground is blue and its drawing belongs there; moving the close to ink to satisfy the palette was the wrong way round.

**The mistake it prevents.** Moving a close off blue, or dropping its drawing, only because ch05 has no blue palette.

**How to apply.** On blue the paper palette is inverted by its own logic rather than invented: lines and the one solid plane in paper, plane faces in the ground's blue, detail on the solid plane in blue, the dot grid in paper at 30%, peridot and ink unchanged (scripts/enquiry-sketch-to-shipped.mjs). ch05 §4a's "Palette by ground" gains a blue column at the next /dbf-update.
