Which action row a frame takes (Line, Button or QR) is decided by its layout. The action line names
what is on the other side, in the reader's words, and it may be an imperative: READ THE FULL CASE,
SEE SIX OPEN ROLES, RESERVE A SEAT · 14 OCTOBER.

**Why.** The layout knows whether it has room for a destination and an action side by side or
carries a date and a code; tying the row to the family would force misfits. An imperative is the
clearest way to name one action.

**The mistake it prevents.** Banning imperatives as "salesy" and writing vague labels, or putting
platform instructions on the canvas: "link in bio", "swipe up", "tap the link" are never allowed,
because the PNG outlives the link's location.

**How to apply.** One action per set; the closer's last content row carries it.
