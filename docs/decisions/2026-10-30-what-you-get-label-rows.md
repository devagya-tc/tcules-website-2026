What you get, who does the work, what we need from you and where our part stops are four peer parts of one
offer, one pattern on every service page. Each part's title is the one the page's copy authors.

**The arrangement.** Four rows, each the part's name in a label column and its content beside it from 1024
(over it below). Row 1 is the deliverables as a strip of drawn items (five across at the 1240 cap, three then
two from 1024 to 1439). Rows 2 to 4 are compact: a 16:9 drawing beside its words. The label to content seam is
`section` (96, a split), row 1 to the term rows `column` (64, different kinds), between term rows `stack` (48).

**All flat.** The studio photograph leaves the band (one-register-per-section): every visual is a Sketch.
"Who does the work" draws our team frame with the senior designer lit and the account lead, the roles always in
the team solid and routed in, the specialists who join when needed dashed. "What we need" is the handover,
"where our part stops" our check on its own card beside their panel, no route into it. Each drawing keeps
every mark inside a frame (flat-graphics-sit-in-frames) and shows its title's one idea.

**No numbers, no hairlines, no containers.** Rows are separated by Layout seams alone; sequence is the reading
order. A ruled text-only row is disliked, so the mockup's row numbers and rules are not built.

**Why.** The mockup the owner reviewed read as one offer answering four questions with equal weight, and a label column gives each question its own place without a box or a rule.

**The mistake it prevents.** The terms read as small print under the deliverables, or as a photograph among
drawings.

**How to apply.** One pattern, fed by each page's copy; a page whose drawings are missing keeps the sheet.
Owner approves the arrangement in the browser before this leaves proposed.
