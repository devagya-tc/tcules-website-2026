A QR frame is paper or paper-deep, with the white code block ruled by a 1px hairline in the ground's
rule ink so it reads as an object. Ink remains possible as the set's one ink frame, where the block
needs no rule.

**Why.** The old rule, "a frame with a QR: ink, whatever the family", stopped working once ink became
a reserved ground (24 Sep). The code must stay on white to scan; on a light ground the hairline is
what separates the block from the frame.

**The mistake it prevents.** A QR frame defaulting to ink and spending the set's one reserved frame,
or an unruled white block that melts into paper.

**How to apply.** QR block per A8.10: 148 or 172px square, white, radius 0, one mono line saying what
scanning does. Scan it from the exported file.
