Paper is the primary social ground and paper-deep the secondary; paper carries more than half of
every set. Ink and brandeis-500 are reserved: one frame per set at most, the hinge or a single frame
the brief argues. The hinge may change ground to do its job: paper-deep, ink or brandeis-500. One
hinge per set, from frame three onward.

**Why.** The owner's correction: the rule is that paper dominates, not that the ground never
changes. The hinge turns the argument, and a change of ground is a legitimate way to mark the turn
once.

**The mistake it prevents.** Reading the C1 reservation as "the hinge never changes ground", or the
opposite: two ink or blue frames in one set, or alternating hinges.

**How to apply.** Count frames: paper above half, at most one ink or blue frame. Never two frames on
ink or blue in one set.
