Home's "Start where your product is: brand new, or already in use" band is "one drawing, two states":
the section head as authored, then ONE wide isometric illustration across the content column showing the
SAME product in two states (on the left a first release taking shape, on the right the established
product in use at scale, extended and taken further), "one continuous drawing, one base/ground line, not
two pictures". Under it, two EQUAL columns, each under its half of the drawing: the authored path title
(serif, the path's hero), its paragraphs, then its two or three service links. No container, no tinted
ground, no hairlines, no tabs or sliders. On a phone the drawing stays one piece, scaled, above the two
columns stacked.

**Why.** The owner chose the direction from the references: Figma's home, one wide image under two
equal text columns, and Vanta's "Built for you", one drawn subject shown in a different setting per path.
Two separate pictures read as two products; one product in two states is what the band says ("Start
where your product is").

**The mistake it prevents.** GatewayPaths as built on feat/new-copy: a separate SituationFigure per
path, borrowed from the six-situation board's service drawings (new-product, product-design), in a 180px
box over each path, so the band showed two unrelated scenes for one product.

**How to apply.** `GatewayPaths` takes the drawing once, as `visual`, a `block` above the paths; a path
no longer carries its own. The drawing is `SituationFigure name="product-paths" fit="width"`, static,
framed on its own extent, its message line and devices written above it in
scripts/illustrations/situations.mjs and checked row by row against ch05 §4a–§4b. It is 2.2:1 overall,
outside the 0.75–1.35 band of `2026-09-28-illustration-aspect-limit` because the owner asked for one wide
drawing; each half (one state, over one column) is 1.1:1, inside it. That band still holds for every
single-subject illustration. Content words are never changed, and paper-deep stays the case cards' alone
(`2026-10-29-paper-deep-is-for-case-cards`).
