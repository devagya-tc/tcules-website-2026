Every illustration is judged by eight gates, in three groups.

**Meaning.** No exceptions. Failing either gate means the concept is replaced, not redrawn.
1. **One event.** The message can be written as "[one thing] goes from [A] to [B]". Consistency or "holds its shape" on its own is not an event.
2. **A claim that could be false.** It shows something the reader knows usually goes wrong, and says it goes right. A sentence any agency could put on its own hero fails.

**Legibility.** Decided by drawing and simplifying.

3. **The ends carry it.** Crop to the first and last layer and the read-back survives.
4. **One subject.** Everything else is ground, with at most two supporting marks per plane. Nothing draws the eye as strongly as the item.
5. **The same thing changes state, not kind.**
6. **Common grammar only.** Up, dashed, solid, aligned, scattered and unequal size. A device that needs a label to be understood fails.
7. **The stranger test.** Someone who hasn't seen the message, with labels off, names the change within five seconds.

**Set.**

8. **Its own silhouette.** In a thumbnail row with the other heroes it has a different construction. The stack-plus-thread construction belongs to the Intent Stack.

The Intent Stack (ch05 §4c) is the reference that passes all eight.

**Why.** The owner: "i'm not sure what it means for an illustration to be meaningful enough that it passes … we need a definition of what we accept and what we don't." The Intent Stack is the only approved illustration, and nobody could say why it works and the others don't. Concepts were built by mapping a page's content onto the drawing, one plane per section and one device per noun. A page is a list, so the drawings came out "too busy with so many items" and needed labels to explain themselves. The owner wants "one clear idea" and a visual that is "very straightforward".

**The mistake it prevents.**
- Design Systems round 5. Three concepts each carried several ideas plus devices that need explaining: an owner's seat, an exceptions shelf, a sorting gate. They failed read-back.
- Design Systems round 5, the "Five parts, held in register" mock. Nothing happens in it, the pipes compete with the item, and it "reads too much like the intent stack".
- The six shipped service heroes, re-tested on 22 Oct. None passes.
  - The Product Design hero has no event, an item that changes kind, and a labelled model panel.
  - Design Systems is legible but says nothing: "consistent everywhere".
  - Design Engineering is indistinguishable from Design Systems.
  - Modernisation and AI Product UX reuse the stack-plus-thread construction.

**How to apply.**
- Write the event first and test gates 1–2 before any construction is chosen.
- Choose the construction second, and check it against the other heroes as one row of thumbnails (gate 8).
- Gates 3–7 are checked on the mock, with labels off, before it is shown.
- A concept review reports the gate it fails, not a general impression.
- This refines [An illustration's concept comes from its page's argument](2026-09-28-illustration-concept-from-page-argument.md): the page's argument still supplies the concept, and gate 1 makes it one event.
- ch05 §4 has a read-back rule but no gates.
