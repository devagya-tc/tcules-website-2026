Whether a piece of content gets a flat drawing or an isometric illustration is answered by two questions:

1. **Where does the idea live?** On a surface (a screen, a flow through it, a before and after, states
   over time), it is drawn flat. A thing (layers built on layers, an item carried somewhere, a system
   seen from outside) is drawn isometric. Complexity does not decide it: the Reasons ideas are simple,
   and they are objects.
2. **What sits next to it?** Two neighbouring bands with drawings never share a style. When the content
   allows either, the page's rhythm decides. Every drawing is made new for its item, so either style is
   always available.

No rule ties a style to a component.

**Why.** Devagya, 22 Oct: "we will need more rules around what type of content uses what type of
graphic", after the visual iterations. The two questions explain every choice made so far (the bento
and covers flat, the Why Tcules set isometric) without naming a component.

**The mistake it prevents.** Reaching for a Sketch because the slot is a card, or an isometric because
the slot is large. `2026-10-15-sketch-for-small-visuals` is the one per-component rule left, and this
would replace it once active. Two drawing bands in the same style back to back.

**How to apply.** Ask both questions before drawing anything for a page, and write the answer in the
page's mapping. Read with the proposed `2026-10-25-two-graphic-tiers-no-monochrome` (on branch
`design/reasons-no-peridot`), which keeps two tiers: flat graphics and peridot isometrics.
