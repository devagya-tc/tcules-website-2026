A service illustration shows what is specific to Tcules, its selling point, and never a process. It
can't be a generic isometric illustration that "anybody can draw", and it never looks like an
architectural drawing of a building. The concept is well defined and detailed. The drawing is simple, clear and
straightforward.

**Why.** Jatin: illustrations "can't be generic isometric illustrations. anybody can draw it. what makes it
tcules specific?" Devagya, ruling out process references: "we don't want to show process, we want to show
what's our unique selling point."

**The mistake it prevents.**
- The Software Development hero reads as a building: a walled cube with floors.
- The Design Systems "Five parts" mock reads as shelving or a car park.
- The process-line and tab-label ideas taken from the 22 Oct references (`docs/research/illustration-references.md`, R4 and R6) were rejected for this reason.

**How to apply.**
- The detail goes into the concept, written out before drawing. The drawing stays simple.
- Test every concept against "could another studio put this on its own page?" If yes, it fails. This sits alongside gate 2 in `2026-10-22-illustration-acceptance-gates`.
- No floors, walls or storeys: no construction that reads as a building.
