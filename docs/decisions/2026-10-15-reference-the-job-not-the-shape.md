Before collecting references, write down what the content does (who reads it, what decision it serves, what each item argues), then find references doing that job. "It's not just the shape of content but also WHAT the content is trying to do."

**Why.** The research was briefed as a "situation selector", so 14 of 16 references were picking mechanics (hover lists, sentence forms, tabs) that treat the items as menu labels. The owner kept only Cuberto's service stack and Apple's "Get to know iPhone", where each item is a short pitch with room to argue.

**The mistake it prevents.** A research brief that names the old component's mechanic and returns references whose items are labels when ours are arguments.

**How to apply.** The brief to a research agent states the job and the content, not the old component, and is checked against the logged rulings first (one fold, gateways serve the ICP's decision, no hidden cards).
