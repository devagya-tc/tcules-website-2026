On the dotted plate only frames sit: screens, cards, documents. People, icons, checks and marks live inside a frame,
never loose on the dots. Routes (lines with arrowheads) between frames are allowed on the plate. A drawing in the page
body depicts what its TITLE says, not the details of its body text.

**Why.** Loose marks on the plate (the person and arrow in "A tested brief") read as clutter; drawings that chased the
body copy became busy and unreadable at card size.

**The mistake it prevents.** A plate scattered with icons, and a drawing that illustrates a sentence nobody reads first.

**How to apply.** Check every scene: list its loose marks and move each into a frame; name the title's one idea and
draw that.
