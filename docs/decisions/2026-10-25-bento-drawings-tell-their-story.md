The six service drawings in the home What we do bento are the **story** drawings (`Sketch story`):
redrawn to the flat-graphics grammar and drawn in two states, **the problem at rest and solved when
the tile is hovered or focused**, in one move on the tile's own hover curve, there and back.

- Product design: both branches trace out of the decision and the decision fills.
- Product modernisation: the rebuild runs across and the clean screen's block lights.
- Design systems: the route traces into the screen and the component lands in its slot.
- AI product UX: the cursor moves onto Accept and it fills.
- Design engineering: the route reaches the code and the line of code lights.
- Software development: the bus traces from all three layers and the release lights.

**Why.** "Not all of them need to have motion... maybe in the services card we could do some hover
animation?" The tile already moves on hover; the drawing now moves with it, telling the service's
story instead of sitting still.

**The mistake it prevents.** Animating every flat drawing (article covers do not move), an entrance
animation on a card grid (parked until a page has a large drawing standing alone), and a hover
built from keyframes or replays rather than one reversible move.

**How to apply.** Only the bento passes `story`; every other Sketch use keeps today's drawings until
the grammar (`docs/research/flat-graphics-grammar.md`, not yet a ruling) is rolled out. The drawing at
rest is the problem by design, which is also what a phone shows. Built on
`2026-10-19-hover-is-a-move` and `2026-10-19-animated-drawing-story-first`; the light weight stays
(`2026-10-19-bento-sketches-are-light`).
