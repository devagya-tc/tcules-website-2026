Iconography

One set, one style, one colour. Icons are solid glyphs that take the ink around them. They mark, point and confirm; they never carry a meaning the words beside them do not already carry.

Choosing a glyph

One meaning, one glyph

Solar has several glyphs for most ideas. The brand uses one for each, everywhere, in every medium. Pick by silhouette at small size: if two glyphs read alike at 16px, the reader stops trusting either. These are the fixed choices for the meanings that recur.

Size

Seven sizes

Seven sizes across every medium. An icon is always one of these; it is never scaled to fit a space. Beside text, the icon matches the cap height of the line it sits on; standing alone, it takes the next step up.

Colour

The current ink

An icon has no colour of its own. It takes the ink of the text beside it, so it is right on any of the four grounds without a decision being made.

paper · neutral-900
tint · neutral-900
ink · white
blue · white
Do
  • Beside body text, body ink. Beside a caption, the caption's muted ink.
  • On a solid fill, the label's colour: white on blue, paper on ink.
  • On a peridot highlight, neutral-950, like the text.
Don't
  • Blue on an icon beside neutral text. The accent is for actions; an icon is not an action.
  • Peridot on an icon, ever.
  • Two tones, gradients or opacity. One fill, the current ink.
With text

Pairing

Trailing for movement, leading for a named action or a status. The gap is a quarter of the icon’s size in a button, half its size in a line of text. The icon centres on the x-height of the text beside it, not on the line box.

Meaning

Decorative by default

Beside a label

The common case, in any medium. The words carry the meaning; on screen the icon is hidden from assistive technology, and in print it needs no explanation.

Alone

Only when the meaning is unambiguous without words: a close cross, a menu, a search glass. On screen it then carries an accessible name and a hit area of at least 44px, however small the glyph.

Density

One icon per line, per action, per tile, per slide bullet. A line with two glyphs asks the reader to decode instead of read.

Never in running text or headings

Icons mark actions, list rows and statuses. They do not decorate a heading, replace a bullet, or stand in for a word in a sentence.
