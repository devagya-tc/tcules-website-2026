Social media

A post is one composed graphic carrying one idea, published with a caption. Every post is built the same way: an identity row, one to four content rows, and an action row, on a 1080 canvas with an 80 margin and three gap values. What varies is how many content rows the frame carries, which patterns they take, and — rarely — whether a frame leaves paper (A4). A share preview is the separate case — one image that travels with a tcules.com URL into a link card — and it is in Part C.

Colour is by token and value; the ramps live in chapter 02. Imagery follows chapter 05.

Scope and inheritance

What this chapter owns

Composed graphics

Anything exported as one image that carries type: a single post, a carousel frame, a thumbnail, a cover. Chapter 05's rule against text baked into an image applies to an image box inside the graphic, and the graphic itself is type by design.

Inherited

Three type families, the warm neutrals, "blue means clickable" — read on a composed graphic as blue marks the action (A1.02) — the surface model, and chapter 05's isometric-line illustration direction. Where a 1080 canvas needs a value the site has no use for — a counter, a QR block, a swipe arrow — this chapter is the authority.

Locked shell

The canvas, the 80 margin, the gap ladder, the type ladder and the identity row hold on every graphic. They are settled, so the work goes into the ground, the patterns and the illustration.

Export scale

Composed graphics export at their platform master. Chapter 05's 2560px floor covers on-page imagery and does not apply here.

Anatomy

Every frame is a single grid: an identity row on a first frame, one to four content rows, and an action row. Exactly one row grows and shrinks — the illustration, or the headline block — and it is set to minmax(0,1fr) so a tall illustration shrinks rather than pushing the action row off the canvas. Every other row is its own height.

The ceiling is a cut. Copy that runs past its ceiling loses words. There is no step-down and no resize: a size that moves to accommodate a sentence stops being a ladder, and one frame in a set then reads at a different rank from the others. The hero is the one slot with a choice of rung, and the choice is made on the character count before the copy is set: 88 to 40 characters, 72 to 55, and 64 where the hero sits in a split column beside an illustration or an image plate. On a share preview the 64 rung sits in the 600px type column of layouts D and P, where it holds 3 lines · 50 characters; copy past it is cut, not accommodated (C3). The rung is picked once for a set, so every frame in it still reads at the same rank. On square, a three-line hero takes 72 by that rule rather than by exception.

On the square canvas every ceiling but the hero's loses a line. The canvas drops 270px against portrait, so the support runs to one line and a caption to two. The hero keeps its rung's ceiling, and a hero past 88's 40 characters takes 72 for the set. Check the ceiling against the canvas being exported.

Ten slots, and no measure. A line wraps at the live width and the character ceiling is what stops it, so there is one number to check rather than a measure and a word count that can contradict each other. Display copy keeps text-wrap: balance so a two-line hero breaks evenly.

Converting a portrait to a square

The live width is 920px on both canvases, so nothing reflows and no line rewraps. Only height is short: the content block falls from 1023px to 753px, a deficit of 270px. That is the whole problem, and it is a smaller problem than it looks.

Nothing is shrunk. The 270px is found in the order below, and content that does not fit the square's ceilings moves to the caption. No size steps off the ladder to keep a word on the frame.

Stop at the first step that clears the deficit, and do not skip one: a frame that drops its support line while the illustration is still at its portrait size has taken 69px where 149px was available, and lost a sentence for nothing.

One crop, both canvases. The image box changes height between formats — 640px to 440px — and its crop does not. A second crop for a second format makes two different images out of one, and nothing downstream records which is current. Where a 440px box cannot hold the crop the portrait box held, the crop was too tight to begin with.

The cover exception. A cover carrying an illustration, a hero, a support line and a terms row has no slack to find, and the four steps above will strip it to something thinner than it should be. A cover's hero may be written shorter for square instead — which makes it a different frame, authored for the canvas, briefed as such and reviewed on its own. It is not a conversion, and this is the only place the permission exists.

The story canvas. The 240px bands top and bottom belong to the platform — its avatar and progress bar above, its reply field and sticker tray below. Nothing readable enters them and neither does a hairline, which leaves 920 × 1280px to work in. There is no full-canvas alternative: the bands are present on every story, on every platform, so a layout drawn to the 1080 × 1920 edge is simply one whose first and last 240px are covered. The type sizes hold, because the canvas is still 1080px wide; only the ceilings move, and they tighten rather than loosen. A story is looked at for about five seconds and tapped through, so it takes one or two content rows and a hero of two lines.

Where the link sticker goes. The poster places it on the canvas, not in the chrome, and the platform's reply field covers the bottom 240px — so a sticker parked down there is half hidden. It sits inside the live area, and the frame reserves 144px at the foot for it: 120px for the sticker, a 24px bind above. It is then the frame's action: the action row carries the mark and may add a mono action line naming what the sticker opens. No Button: one link affordance per frame (B2).

The reshare still stands. A square frame centred on its own ground extended to 1920px is how feed work goes into a story. It is not re-laid out and not scaled up: the square stays 1080 × 1080px and the ground grows around it. A frame carrying client work is not reshared to a story.

The gap ladder

Three rungs, each named for what it holds together. Every distance in a frame, vertical or horizontal, is one of the three. A distance of 34px is a mistake, not a nuance.

RungValueHolds
Bind24pxTwo items that read as one thing: hero and support, label and date, the mark and its hairline. Also the shell against the content block, so the identity and action rows sit tight and the content owns the air that is left.
Row48pxOne content row against the next. Twice the bind, so which items belong together is unambiguous at feed size.
Field80pxThe margin. No interior distance exceeds it.

Two to a group. A bind holds two items. A third starts a new row at 48px, with one exception: a notation line — terms, a relationship line, an n-and-window line — may join a pair as a third member at the bind, because it reads as annotation rather than as content. Three items at equal spacing is not a group; it is a list that has lost its hierarchy.

Off the ladder. Two things are not the frame's distances and are not on the ladder: a component's own internal spacing (the QR block, a Button's padding), and the leading inside one text object (a name over its role, a row title over its line). The ladder governs the distances between rows and between the members of a row.

A5 · The marked phrase

A rectangle drawn behind the words, with clear space on every side

The band is a drawn object with its own geometry. It clears the tallest and deepest letter the phrase actually contains by .09em and runs .14em past the first and last letter, so the air reads equal whatever the word is. The text takes neutral-950; the band takes peridot-500.

Part E

Working with AI

A model can cut a headline to a ceiling, propose an assembly, draft a caption, check contrast and generate an illustration to a written brief. It cannot decide what Tcules thinks, and it cannot see the things in A5 and A8.

E1 · The never list

Twelve things AI must never do on a Tcules graphic

Each one has been seen. A model asked to "make it look right" will do most of them.

№Never
01Invent a claim, a figure, a date or a client name. Every number arrives from the brief with its scope attached. A brief with no figure produces a frame with no figure.
02Write the hero. It may cut, re-order and shorten the studio's own words to fit a ceiling. Raising the claim, sharpening it, or making it more confident than the source is out.
03Add a fourth type family, a fifth ground, a gradient, a shadow (other than a cut-out's, chapter 05 §2a), a radius or an emoji. A cut-out's contrast panel (chapter 05 §2a) is part of its image box, not a ground. Empty is a layout, not a gap.
04Build the peridot band as a text background. It is the drawn rectangle in A5, at the geometry for the letters the phrase actually contains.
05Spend more than two peridot marks in a set — an illustration spends one, a marked phrase one — put two on adjacent frames, or mark something it decided was important.
06Put type over an image box, or rescue contrast with a scrim, a blur or a text shadow.
07Exceed a ceiling in A3, or set an in-between size to make copy fit. Cut the copy and say what was cut.
08Move the mark out of a margin corner, resize it, or use the colour-bar version. Monochrome, once per canvas: 40px in a first frame's header, 24px in a content frame's footer (A6); 32px in a share preview's action row (C2), and 40px top-left on a thumbnail and on layout F (D1). Absent only from an uncomposed photograph and the LinkedIn company cover; the profile picture is the logomark (D1).
09Generate a photograph of a person, a team or a workspace and present it as Tcules. Photographs are shot in-studio per chapter 05 §3.
10Reuse an illustration across topics, or ship one without its message line. Chapter 05 §4 requires a written message and a read-back before an illustration is placed.
11Put a QR on anything but white, ship its block unruled on paper or paper-deep, or ship one without the line saying what scanning does. Scan it from the exported file.
12Ship without alt text, or write alt text describing the layout.

And one positive obligation: report the decisions. A frame that arrives without a list of what was chosen and what was cut can only be re-done, not reviewed.
