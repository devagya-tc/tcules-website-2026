Colour

Three colour families, the paper pair and a small status set; four grounds. Colour is never chosen freely: a ground is set once, and everything on it reads its ink from that ground.

Overview

The families and what each means

FamilyBaseRole
Brandeisbrandeis-500The primary. Actions, links, the accent, and the one saturated ground. Where the eye is meant to go, and therefore rationed.
Neutralneutral-900Ink, muted ink, labels, rules and the dark ground. A warm grey, so it sits with paper rather than against it.
Peridotperidot-500The highlight. Two uses only: a marked phrase in text, and the carried item inside an illustration with the concept chip's block that points at it, which count together as one mark (chapter 05 §4b), at the steps chapter 05 §4a sets. Never text, never a ground, never a button, and never in the wordmark. On social a set spends two peridot marks at most, a marked phrase or an illustration each, never on adjacent frames (chapter 10 §A1.03); a share preview spends one, a marked phrase or an illustration's carried item, never both (chapter 10 §C2).
PaperpaperThe default ground, and its deeper sibling for a band whose content needs to sit apart. Off-white on purpose; pure white is ink on dark grounds, not a ground itself.
Statusdanger-600Validation and confirmation. Never decorative, never a brand accent.

Palette · The wordmark on grounds

Two colours at most, per ground

The wordmark has two coloured parts: the letters and the bar. On paper at marketing scale the letters stay neutral-950 and the bar is brandeis-500. In UI chrome — nav, footer, favicon and anything at that size, where the blue thins out — the whole wordmark is neutral-950. On blue and on ink it is white throughout. Social media uses the monochrome wordmark on every ground (chapter 10 §A6). Chapter 01 carries the suite, clear space and minimum sizes.

Surface model

Four grounds

A page, a deck or a post is a stack of bands. Each band declares one ground, and the eight roles below are remapped for it. Nothing inside a band chooses a colour; it reads a role. This is what makes one button correct on every ground without being told where it is.

Nested grounds. Something inside a band never knows which band it is in. A tint block between two ink bands renders the tint column throughout. Set the ground once, on the band; never set a colour on anything inside it.

Semantic roles

What each role is for

RoleMeaning
groundThe band's fill. The only colour a band paints.
inkPrimary text: headings, body, labels on neutral solids.
ink-mutedSecondary text that is still read: notes, bylines, captions.
ink-labelMono labels: eyebrows, metadata, table heads. Quieter than muted on paper, louder on tint.
ruleThe hairline that separates, closes or edges. Decorative, and held below 3:1 on purpose.
rule-strongA rule that carries structure: a table head, an open state, the top of a list.
accentThe one saturated element in a band: a solid button, an active marker.
linkInline link ink and its hover.
Proportion

How much of each

Measured across a whole page or deck, not one screen. The balance is what makes blue read as an accent rather than a theme.

GroundShareRule
Paper~70%The default. Every page opens on paper, and closes on paper or tint before the ink footer.
Tint~20%Earned by content, never alternated for rhythm: a band that needs to sit apart from the page — a proof block, a form, a reference table. Never two tint bands adjacent.
Ink~5–10%The footer, and at most one pivot band mid-page.
Blue≤5%One band per page at most, usually the ask. Never adjacent to an ink band.
Peridot≤1%A marked phrase, or an illustration's carried item and its concept chip's block, and nothing else. If it reads as an area, there is too much.

Pairing

What may sit on each ground

Every legal pairing, per ground, with its contrast ratio. Text is held to 4.5:1, with two exemptions: disabled text, which is meant to recede, and a list's row number, which is decoration because the row's title carries the meaning. Rules, the wordmark bar and button grounds are decoration or fills and are exempt; they are listed so the set is complete. Anything not shown as legal is not legal.
