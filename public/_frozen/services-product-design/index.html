Product design for software that's easy to use, however complex it gets

We design SaaS and complex software that customers understand quickly and engineers can build with fewer open questions. Research, workflows, interface design, prototyping and testing, carried into code when you need it.

Start a Product Design enquiry

What you get

  • A product that needs less explaining.

    Workflows that match how your users actually work, so they find their way without a manual.

  • Complex rules, simple screens.

    Roles, permissions and exceptions are worked out before they reach the interface, long before customers trip over them.

  • Fewer surprises in development.

    Designs that cover the awkward cases too, so your engineers spend their time building instead of guessing.

  • An interface that feels finished.

    Loading, empty and error states are designed too, so nothing looks half-built when real data arrives.

Where we start depends on what's going wrong

  • “Customers keep getting stuck.”

    We watch how people really use the product, then redesign the workflows that trip them up.

  • “The product has grown messy.”

    We reorganise its structure and navigation, so people stop hunting for features they know are in there somewhere.

  • “We want to test an idea before building it.”

    We prototype it and put it in front of real users.

  • “Every screen looks slightly different.”

    We define shared patterns your team can reuse.

  • “What ships doesn’t match the design.”

    We work in code with your developers until it does.

Example

Dealpath

Read the Dealpath case

  1. The problem.

    Dealpath helps real estate investment teams manage their deal pipeline. Its "Add to pipeline" button always started a new deal, even when that property was already in the pipeline.

  2. What we did.

    We started with a wider review of the product, then redesigned this workflow so teams search for an existing deal before creating a new one. We changed the listing card and the pipeline action, and kept the rest of the workspace as users knew it.

  3. The result.

    A redesigned flow that catches a duplicate deal before it is created, kept tight enough for Dealpath's own engineers to build without reworking the rest of the workspace.

More complex products we have made easier to use

  • Doodle.

    One interview showed that people make time for an important meeting even when their calendar says they are busy. We built meeting types and their priority into the design of the scheduling MVP.

  • Scissors.

    Staffing software where onboarding, shortlisting, talent pools and job creation each ran as a separate sequence. We redesigned all four around one connected model of clients, candidates and jobs, so details entered once carry through the rest of the work.

  • Eden AI.

    Three ways to start a workflow (template, AI help or blank page) brought together in one starting point.

  • Auxentios.

    In Auxentios' industrial quoting product, six specialist roles contribute to each quote. After eight user interviews, we designed one shared quoting workflow for all of them.

Frequently asked questions

Is Product Design the same as UI/UX design?

UI and UX design are part of it. Product Design also covers the research, structure and workflows behind the screens, and it can carry on into code.

Can we start with one feature or workflow?

Yes. One workflow or feature is a complete project in its own right, and often the best place to start.

What do we receive at the end?

Whatever the work needs: research findings, user flows and site structure, interface designs, clickable or coded prototypes, usability test results, reusable design patterns and guidance for your developers. We agree the outputs before we start.

How much research do we need?

Only as much as the problem needs. That might be a handful of user interviews, your analytics and support tickets, or research you already have.

What will you need from us?

Access to the product or prototype, any existing designs, and time with the people who know your users and workflows best.

Can you hand over to our developers, or do you build it?

Either. We can hand your developers designs that are ready to build and stay on to review what they ship, or our own engineers can build it.

When should we start with an audit instead?

When you know something is wrong but not what. A UX & Product Audit finds the cause before you spend money redesigning the wrong thing.
