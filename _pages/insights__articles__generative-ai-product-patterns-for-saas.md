# Seven generative AI product patterns for SaaS and the controls each needs

By [Shoghi Bagul](/insights/authors/shoghi-bagul) · Published 2 May 2025 · Updated 26 October 2026 · 8 min read

This article describes seven ways to put generative AI into a SaaS product, what to design for in each, and how to test it. Start with the part of the user's work where AI could help: finding information, developing a draft, judging a result or carrying out a task. Then decide what the system needs to know and what it may do.

The patterns can share a model or a chat surface and still need different evidence, controls and tests.

**Key takeaways**

- Seven patterns put generative AI into a SaaS product: generate, transform, retrieve and synthesise, recommend, predict or detect, orchestrate, and act.
- Each pattern needs its own evidence, controls and tests, even when several share a model or a chat surface.
- Control should grow with authority. Editing suits a draft, while delegated action needs permission, a stop condition and recovery.
- Chat suits open questions and exploration. Comparing, repeating, tracking progress and bulk work usually need structured records and previews.
- Choose the narrowest pattern that creates value, and describe what happens when the AI cannot help.

## 1. AI can generate a candidate for a person to judge {#1-generate-a-candidate}

The system drafts text, structure, code, media or a plan. The user remains the author or approver.

**Design for:** editable output, source constraints, variations, keeping what the user typed, and a clear saved or unsaved status.

**Evaluate:** usefulness, correction effort, omissions, and whether the draft fits the intended audience or system.

## 2. AI can transform material a person provides {#2-transform-supplied-material}

The system summarises, rewrites, translates, classifies or extracts from bounded input.

**Design for:** source coverage, changed meaning, retained terminology, traceability, and side-by-side review where the consequence warrants it.

**Evaluate:** fidelity to the source, missing material and the cost of verification.

## 3. AI can retrieve sources and synthesise an answer {#3-retrieve-and-synthesise}

The system finds relevant material and builds an answer. The [AI Search and Recommendation service](/services/ai-product-ux/ai-search-recommendation) turns this into a product interaction, with explicit decisions about the body of sources, the evidence and recovery.

**Design for:** source identity, passage support, freshness, access rights and a route from the synthesis back to the evidence.

**Evaluate:** retrieval quality separately from answer quality. A fluent answer can hide a missing source.

## 4. AI can recommend an option without making the decision {#4-recommend}

The system ranks options or proposes a next move.

**Design for:** the basis of the recommendation, alternatives, the objective, trade-offs and the ability to adjust the criteria.

**Evaluate:** decision quality, calibration and the effect on different users, beyond click-through.

## 5. AI can predict or detect a condition in product data {#5-predict-or-detect}

The system estimates an outcome or identifies a pattern in data.

**Design for:** time window, data coverage, thresholds, the consequence of false positives and false negatives, and escalation.

**Evaluate:** performance in the real operating population, and what happens around the threshold.

## 6. AI can orchestrate several steps across a workflow {#6-orchestrate-a-workflow}

The system turns an objective into steps across tools, records or people.

**Design for:** a visible plan, permissions and the status of each step. Also design for dependencies, interruption, retry and partial completion.

**Evaluate:** correct completion, recovery effort, and whether the user can understand what changed.

## 7. AI can act with delegated authority {#7-act-with-delegated-authority}

The system changes records, sends messages, moves money, deploys code or takes another consequential action.

**Design for:** bounded authority, preview, confirmation, the least access needed, an audit history, stop conditions and recovery.

**Evaluate:** safe completion and failure handling. Task speed alone cannot show that an action was safe.

## AI UX design patterns compared: authority, uncertainty and control {#each-pattern-needs-a-different-control}

| Pattern | Typical authority | Primary uncertainty | Essential control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generate | Suggest | Quality and fit | Edit or discard |
| Transform | Suggest | Fidelity | Compare with source |
| Retrieve and synthesise | Inform | Coverage and support | Inspect sources |
| Recommend | Influence | Objective and ranking | Alternatives and criteria |
| Predict or detect | Flag | Error distribution | Threshold and escalation |
| Orchestrate | Prepare or execute steps | Plan and dependency | Step-level review and status |
| Act | Commit | Consequence | Permission, stop and recovery |

## Retrieval vs generation UX: when AI should generate or retrieve {#retrieval-vs-generation-ux}

Many GenAI features for SaaS mix the two, and people judge them differently. Retrieved material is checked against its source. Generated material is judged as a draft.

| | Retrieve and synthesise | Generate |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Answers that already exist in sources people trust: policies, records, documentation | New material the person will edit and own: a draft, a plan, a first structure |
| Main risk | A fluent answer that hides a missing or outdated source | A plausible draft that misses what the audience or system needs |
| What the interface shows | Which sources were used, and a route back to each passage | What the model wrote, what the person changed and what is still unsaved |

When one feature does both, mark which parts came from sources and which the model wrote.

On [Eden AI](/case-studies/eden-ai-workflow-builder-search-redesign), one search box served both: finding a template or capability, or moving into AI-assisted mode. The design kept them as two separate moves you can see, with Tab switching into AI help. Preview cards let people check a capability before adding it.

## When chat fits a GenAI feature, and when structure works better {#chat-is-not-the-product-model}

A conversation suits open questions and early exploration, and for some products it is the whole product. It struggles when people need to compare, repeat, keep track of where work stands, act in bulk or review precisely. Many products pair language input with structured records, previews and workflows that remember progress.

## How to choose which AI pattern fits a feature {#how-to-choose}

Begin with the user's decision or the state of their work. Select the narrowest pattern that creates value. Define what the model may influence, how people inspect it and what evidence will justify wider authority.

Expect your features to travel down this list over the next few years, from drafting something for a person to judge towards acting with delegated authority. Each move earns its place with a record from the step before: how often people accepted the output, where they corrected it and how a failure was put right. Design that record now, and granting the AI more authority later becomes a product call made on facts.

[AI Product UX](/services/ai-product-ux) carries the chosen pattern through user control and evaluation, so people can rely on the feature in their daily work.

## Plan for useful, partial and failed AI output {#what-happens-when-ai-cannot-help}

For each candidate pattern, describe three situations before you choose it: useful output, uncertain or partial output, and no usable output. The product must say what remains possible in each. That may mean editing a candidate, viewing sources, retrying with changed inputs, reverting an action or continuing through a path without AI.

Delegated action needs the strictest boundary. Name the resources the system may change, the confirmation point, the activity record, the stop condition and the person who owns recovery. A generic model-quality score cannot replace evaluation against the real work and its consequences.

## Generative AI patterns in four products we designed {#patterns-in-products-we-designed}

Four client products we designed show three of these patterns, each with the control it called for.

| Pattern | Product | The control we designed |
|---|---|---|
| **Generate** | [Novus Insights](/case-studies/novus-insights-ai-survey-product-design), AI-assisted questionnaires | In the MVP design, the AI drafts a starting questionnaire. Generated questions sit beside the researcher's edits, and a rewritten question keeps the original visible beneath it |
| **Generate, chosen by a person** | [Omny AI](/case-studies/omny-ai-suggestion-trust-redesign), Amazon listing content | Suggestions wait in their own panel and enter the listing only with a deliberate click. Purple marks AI material, blue marks the person's |
| **Predict or detect** | [BuildTwin](/case-studies/ai-quality-control-expert-review-buildtwin), structural drawing checks | Each AI check links to its source document, names any missing information and can be overruled with a reason that stays on the record |
| **Orchestrate** | [ConnectX](/case-studies/ai-native-building-energy-product-connectx), building energy | A plain-language request is matched to one of eight defined scenarios. Permitted tools fetch the data, and the product assembles the right modules and digital-twin view |

Eden AI, described above, adds retrieval: finding a template or capability, kept apart from AI help. The common thread is that each product put limits on the AI before giving it room. ConnectX's eight scenarios are the clearest case: the model could adapt, but only within rules the product team set.

## Generative AI product patterns: questions teams ask {#faq}

### What are the main generative AI product patterns for SaaS?

Seven cover most features: generate, transform, retrieve and synthesise, recommend, predict or detect, orchestrate, and act. They differ in how much authority the AI holds, from suggesting a draft to committing a change. Several can share one model or chat surface and still need different controls and tests.

### How do I choose which AI pattern fits a feature?

Start from the user's decision or the state of their work, then pick the narrowest pattern that creates value. Define what the model may influence, how people inspect it and what would justify more authority later. Describe what happens when the output is useful, partial or unusable before you commit.

### When should AI generate versus retrieve?

Retrieve when the answer already exists in sources people trust and they need to check it, such as policies, records or documentation. Generate when the person needs a new draft they will edit and own. Many features do both, so show which parts came from sources and which the model wrote.

### Which AI patterns carry the most risk?

Act and orchestrate, because the AI changes records, sends messages or moves money on someone's behalf. They need bounded authority, a preview, confirmation, an activity record, stop conditions and an owner for recovery. Recommend and predict carry quieter risk, since a ranking or threshold can steer choices without anyone noticing.
