*AI trust and control*

# AI suggestions users accept on purpose

Suggestions stay provisional until someone adds them, AI and human work are marked apart, and common fixes get their own buttons instead of another prompt.

[Start a conversation](#enquiry)

*Selected work: Omny AI*

## Where trust in an AI feature breaks down {#recognition}

**Suggestions arrive already accepted.** AI text fills the field before anyone reads it, and mistakes surface after they have gone out.

**Nobody can see who wrote what.** AI output and a person's edits look identical.

**The only fix is another prompt.** Users rephrase again and again to correct one detail they already know is wrong.

**Errors end in "Try again".** Nobody can tell what already happened or whether it is safe to repeat.

## From AI suggestion to saved work {#states}

**Suggested.** Clearly provisional, and set apart from the user's own work.

**Supported.** The sources behind it, and anything it could not check, are one click away.

**Accepted.** The product records who accepted it and when it became part of the real work.

**Correctable.** Users can edit, reject, pick another option, narrow the request or overrule with a reason.

**Recoverable.** Undo where the system can reverse the change, and a clear repair path where it cannot.

## Plan recovery before anything fails {#recovery}

When an AI action goes wrong, the screen should tell users which of these is true:

| What happened | What the user needs |
|---|---|
| Nothing changed | Reassurance, and a way to try again |
| A change happened and can be undone | A one-step undo |
| Part of the task succeeded | A list of what finished, what failed and what can be retried |
| The result is uncertain | A way to check before anyone builds on it |
| The change cannot be reversed | The person or process that can fix it |

Once the system has acted, "Something went wrong" leaves users guessing what to fix.

## Omny AI: users choose what the AI adds {#proof}

Omny AI helps brands that sell on Amazon write their product benefits and use cases. Its original design filled the content area with AI suggestions before the user did anything, and users held Omny accountable for suggestions they had not properly reviewed.

**Blank by default.** We redesigned the screen so the content area starts empty, suggestions sit in a panel beside it and adding one takes a deliberate click.

**Authorship you can see.** Purple marks anything the AI generated, suggested or did automatically. Blue marks anything a person typed, chose or overrode. The same colours run through content, budget allocation and the budget strategy timeline.

Omny reported that users became more aware of how they used the AI, and more forgiving when a suggestion was wrong. Omny AI is one of the Primelis products we design.

## Why Tcules for AI control and recovery {#why-tcules}

**Every control does what it says.** We check undo, retry and override against what your system can really reverse, so the screen never promises a rescue it cannot deliver.

**Common fixes get their own buttons.** Users correct a known detail in one step instead of rewriting their prompt.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### How do you design trust into an AI product?

We start with what users need to know and control: what the AI did, what supports it, what has changed, what they can correct and who has the final say. Trust follows when those answers are easy to find.

### Should AI-generated content look different from human content?

Often, yes. When a person is accountable for what goes out, they need to see at a glance which parts the AI wrote. For Omny AI, one colour marks AI material and another marks human input across the product.

### Will extra controls slow users down?

Only where a pause protects them. A deliberate click before AI content is accepted is a small cost when someone answers for it. Low-risk, reversible changes can apply straight away with an easy undo.
