*MVP and new product design*

# A first release that's narrow, not hollow

We're the designers who turn an idea, or the prototype you built with AI, into a product. We keep what works, design it around your customers and their work, and see it through to a first release ready for real users and real data. Testing ideas in working code early gets you there sooner, without skipping the thinking.

[Start a conversation](#enquiry)

*Selected new-product work: Vovisto · Doodle · Auxentios · ConnectX · Novus Insights*

## Where are you starting? {#recognition}

### An idea you believe in

You know the customer and the problem. We shape the product and decide with you what goes into the first release.

### Designs that need to become a product

The screens show the direction. We work out accounts, roles, data, errors and the full journey, then build it.

### A prototype built with AI tools

A working demo is real progress, and people may already want it. We keep what holds up, fix what's held together with tape and give it a design of its own.

### A new product inside an established company

Something new that has to live alongside existing systems, data and teams. We design and build around the constraints you already have.

## What a good first release needs {#first-release}

| | What it means |
|---|---|
| **One job done end to end** | A valuable task people can finish from start to finish |
| **Clear product rules** | Who can do what, which data is needed, what happens when something goes wrong |
| **An experience people understand** | Clear screens, helpful feedback and a way back from mistakes |
| **Working software** | The frontend, backend and integrations this release needs |
| **A plan to launch and learn** | Testing, release, and a way to see how people use it |

A good MVP does one important job well and shows you what to build next.

## How we work {#delivery-model}

**1. Start from what you know.** Your expertise, research, designs, prototypes and code all count.

**2. Define the product.** Users, roles, core workflows and exactly what the first release includes.

**3. Make it real.** Designs, user tests and coded experiments where they help.

**4. Build and launch.** We build the frontend, backend and integrations, test them and support the release, on our own or alongside your engineers.

We use AI throughout [our own design work](/how-we-work/ai-native-product-design), and people make every call.

## Built it with AI tools? Let's check what holds up {#prototype-readiness}

Prototypes built with AI tools can prove an idea quickly. Before real customers rely on one, it's worth asking:

- Does the full journey work with real users, data and permissions?
- Are the integrations live, or simulated?
- What happens when something fails, or two people edit the same thing?
- Can someone maintain it safely after launch?

We'll tell you which parts can go to customers as they are, and which need work first. You can also book that check on its own, as a [Prototype Readiness Review](/audits/prototype-readiness-review).

## Why build your first product with us {#why-tcules}

**The right product before more screens.** Research and product thinking come first, so you avoid building the wrong thing beautifully.

**Design and build in one team.** Designers and engineers work side by side until release, so the thinking behind the design you approved carries into the code, and any change is discussed with you first.

**Sooner, because we don't start again.** We reuse the research, prototypes and code that still hold up, and try ideas in working code before anyone commits to them.

**Your product, not the defaults.** AI tools and ready-made kits reach for the same colours and layouts, so many new products look alike. We start from your customers' work and words, so the product feels like yours.

## Questions founders ask {#faq}

### Do you design and develop MVPs?

Yes. We design the first release around the job that matters most and can build it, or we can design it for your own engineering team to build.

### How quickly can we launch?

It depends on what the first release must do and what you already have. After a first look at your idea or prototype, we give you a plan with dates. The plan keeps research and user testing in, however tight the date.

### Do we have to throw away what we built with AI?

Usually not. We keep the parts that hold up and restructure or rebuild what can't carry real users and real data. If you want that answer on its own first, a [Prototype Readiness Review](/audits/prototype-readiness-review) gives it to you.

### Will it still look like it was made with AI?

It shouldn't. The screens come from your customers' tasks and your brand, and the tool's stock colours and components go.

### Do we need customer research before we start?

Not always. We start from what you already know about your customers, and suggest new research only when the answer could change what you build.

### Who owns the code and the launch?

We agree it before work starts: who owns the code, who holds access to each system and who runs the launch. The final business and launch calls stay with you.
