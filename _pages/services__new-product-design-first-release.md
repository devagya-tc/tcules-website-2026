# MVP development services that start with what your first release must do

An MVP is for the first paying customer who uses it without you on the call, and the investor meeting where you explain what it leaves out. It is also for a second release built on the first.

Tcules provides MVP development services for founders and product teams: we agree what the first release must do, then design it, build it and support the launch.

## Selected MVP and new product work {#selected-work}

### Novus Insights: AI drafts the questionnaire, the researcher owns it

Novus Insights wanted a survey product where AI writes the starting questionnaire. Interviews showed that the researcher must stand behind every question their audience sees. In the MVP we designed, each generated question shows its prompt, and a rewritten one keeps the AI's original beneath it.

[Read the Novus Insights case](/case-studies/novus-insights-ai-survey-product-design)

### Doodle: research redrew the MVP before the build

Doodle was exploring scheduling for enterprises. An interview revealed that whether someone is free depends on the meeting's priority. The MVP then moved from finding an empty slot to judging whether a meeting will hold, scored on meeting type, priority and tentative plans.

[Read the Doodle case](/case-studies/doodle-case-study-automating-enterprise-meeting-scheduling)

### ConnectX: early ideas to a live AI product

ConnectX came with early concepts for an AI building-energy product. We shaped them into eight scenarios built on the questions its users ask and tried the product in a coded demo with sample data. We then carried it through to a production module, live since late 2025.

[Read the ConnectX case](/case-studies/ai-native-building-energy-product-connectx)

## What you get from our MVP design and development {#what-you-get}

Bring an idea, research, designs or a prototype built with AI tools; we start from what exists. A new product inside an established company is shaped around your existing systems and teams.

**What you receive**

- **Five product choices made with you,** written down where everyone can see them (below).

- **Designs tested before the build,** with people who match your customers trying the core flow.

- **The build,** alongside your engineers or on our own: sign-in, data, payments and the rest.

- **The places new products break:** tested before launch: sign-in and invitations, who sees whose data, payments and refunds, errors and timeouts, and keys kept out of shared code.

- **Launch support** and a plan for later releases.

**Who does the work.** A senior designer leads and designs it, with an account lead as your contact. Specialists join as each part needs them.

**What we need from you.** Whatever exists, from a pitch deck to a working prototype, someone who can make the product calls, and access to people who match your first customers.

**Where our part stops.** You own the code and decide when to launch. Payments and health data raise the bar, so specialist checks such as a security review go into the release plan from the start, each with an agreed owner.

## A first release that's narrow, not hollow {#decisions}

What should an MVP include? Getting a prototype on screen is quick now; getting customers to rely on it is the work, and it starts with five choices. Left to a tool, each becomes a plausible default.

- **Who it's for, and the one job it does.** The customer, the task they can finish, and what counts as done.

- **What the first release leaves out.** Features that wait, written down so they stay out.

- **Who can do what.** Roles, invitations and permissions: who can see, change and approve each thing.

- **Where the data comes from.** What the product stores, takes from your other systems and shows a new account.

- **What happens when something goes wrong.** A declined card, a task left halfway, two people editing one record.

A product with AI at its core adds a sixth: what it may do on its own, and where a person signs off. Our guide to [showing each role why the product is worth using](/insights/guides/saas-onboarding-strategies-to-increase-customer-retention) goes further on the first.

*Illustration, not client work*

A founder wants vet practices to send pet owners a treatment estimate they can approve from their phone. The first release does one job: an owner approves or declines an estimate before treatment starts.

It covers sending, changing, approving and declining one estimate, with prices taken from the practice's existing system and a record the practice can rely on. Deposits, payment plans, insurance claims and reminders wait for later.

## Questions about MVP design and development {#faq}

### What is the difference between MVP design and MVP development?

MVP design decides what the first release does, who it serves and how each screen behaves, failures included. MVP development builds it: sign-in, data, payments and the rest, tested before launch. Tcules does both, or designs the MVP for your own engineers to build.

### What is your MVP design process?

We read what you already have, then define the users, roles, core workflows and what the first release includes and leaves out. We test the riskiest part with designs, user tests or coded experiments before the build, then build, launch and support the release.

### How long does it take to design and build an MVP?

A focused first release often takes a few months from kickoff; larger products and rougher prototypes take longer. Design takes a few weeks and the build a few more. We agree dates with you once we've seen your idea or prototype.

### Can you turn our vibe-coded prototype into a real MVP?

Yes, and how much of the code carries over depends on how sound it is. A prototype from Lovable, Bolt, Replit or Cursor already shows how the product should work, so Tcules keeps what holds up and rebuilds what can't carry real users and data. [Is your vibe-coded app ready for production?](/insights/vibe-coded-app-production-ready)

### Why do apps built with AI tools all look the same?

The tools start from the same kits and fill every gap with the most common choice. Tcules designs from your customers' tasks and your brand instead.

### Do we need customer research before we start?

You can start without it. Tcules builds on what you already know about your customers, and adds research where the riskiest assumption is still untested.

### How much does MVP development cost?

The cost depends on what the first release must do, so Tcules prices MVP work as a defined project. We set the price on the agreed scope of the first release, before work starts. [How pricing works](/how-we-work#engagement-shapes)

### Who owns the code and the launch?

Once the work is paid for, the code and designs Tcules makes for you are yours, and anything from other makers comes with a licence spelled out in the agreement. Before work starts, we agree who holds access and who runs the launch, and the final calls stay with you. [Who owns what](/how-we-work#shared-ownership)
