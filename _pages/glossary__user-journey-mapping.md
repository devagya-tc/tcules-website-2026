*Glossary · User Journey Mapping*

# User journey mapping

User journey mapping represents a person's actions, context, evidence and experience across time so a team can investigate or change a product or service decision. When it matters: A map earns its cost when a decision spans stages no single team sees end to end: onboarding promises, support recovery, renewal.

Without a named decision, mapping becomes wall art.

A useful map might follow a promised onboarding outcome through sales, setup, first value and support. It records observed evidence at each stage and names the decision that evidence could change. Invented stages, emotions and pain points are workshop theatre, not research.

[Workflow design](/glossary/workflow-design) focuses more closely on operational objects, roles, rules and state. [UX Research](/services/product-design/ux-research) can supply the evidence a map needs.

The article on [UX, CX and design thinking](/insights/articles/design-thinking-vs-cx-vs-ux-explained) helps choose the right scope before selecting the mapping method.

## How is user journey mapping used in practice? {#practice}

User journey mapping is useful when a product decision depends on what happens before, during and after the visible interface. The map can connect a person's goal, actions, channels, delays and hand-offs so the team sees where the product is helping, where another system or person takes over and where evidence is still missing.

Tcules uses journey mapping as one research and product-design tool rather than as a required artefact for every engagement. The relevant output depends on the decision the team is trying to make.

## How does user journey mapping differ from workflow design? {#comparison}

The table separates a person's cross-touchpoint experience from the operational structure a product must support.

| User journey mapping | Workflow design |
|---|---|
| Follows a person's goal and experience across touchpoints | Defines how work moves through roles, records, states and exceptions |
| Can include activity outside the software | Focuses on the operational sequence the product must represent or support |
| Helps reveal moments that deserve investigation | Produces product rules and interaction structure for carrying the work |

## Why user journey mapping matters in product work {#next}

A map earns its cost when it changes what the team decides next, which makes it a research question.
