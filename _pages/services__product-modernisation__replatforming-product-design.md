*Replatforming product design*

# Leave the old problems on the old platform

A platform migration is the rare moment a product can change a lot at once. We decide what carries over feature by feature, design the long-wanted fixes and plan what happens to customers' saved work.

[Start a conversation](#enquiry)

*Selected work: Benchmark Gensuite · The Wandering Raven · GT Tools*

## When a platform move becomes a product problem {#recognition}

**"We'll match the old product first and improve it later."** Then the old problems get rebuilt on the new platform.

**"Half our customers are on the new version."** Support has to explain two products at once.

**"Their saved views were lost in the migration."** Filters, reports and settings are what customers notice first.

**"The new platform calls it something else."** Renamed features break help articles, training and habits.

## Decide what carries over, feature by feature {#parity}

"Feature parity" sounds tidy. In practice every feature needs its own answer.

| For each feature | Choose this when |
|---|---|
| **Move it as it is** | Customers rely on it and it still works well |
| **Fix it on the way** | The new platform finally makes a long-wanted change practical |
| **Move it later** | Moving it now would add risk to the migration |
| **Leave it behind** | Too few people use it to justify the cost of moving it |

## Design the in-between period {#coexistence}

For weeks or months, some customers will use the new version while others stay on the old one. We map that period: who sees which version, where their records live, which terms and permissions apply, and how support can tell which version a customer is on.

We also decide what happens to the things customers created, such as saved views, filters, reports, roles and drafts. Some move automatically. Some need translating. Some need the customer to choose.

Your engineers own how the data moves. We define what it should mean to the customer when it arrives.

## Designing within the new platform's limits {#proof}

### Benchmark Gensuite: components for a framework move, tried in code first

Benchmark was moving the shared frontend of its enterprise suite to Bootstrap 5.3. We designed how the shared buttons, forms and controls should look and work on the new framework, and tested them as working prototypes in code.

Starting with the shared components meant each decision was made once for every application in the suite, instead of separately in each.

[Read the Benchmark Gensuite case](/case-studies/benchmark-gensuite-enterprise-suite-modernisation)

### The Wandering Raven: a learning world that works inside Moodle

Learners aged eight to eighteen take The Wandering Raven's creative-writing courses partly through Moodle. We designed the experience, then installed and configured Moodle plugins and built a custom theme, so the characters, prompts and progress badges worked inside the platform.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### What's the difference between replatforming, redesign and a rebuild?

A redesign changes the experience on the same technology. Replatforming moves the product to new technology, so old and new have to coexist for a while. A rebuild replaces the structure itself.

### Can you help before we've chosen the new platform?

Yes. We can map the workflows and customer data the new platform must support, so the choice is made with customers in view. If you're still deciding whether to move at all, start with a [Product Modernisation Assessment](/audits/product-modernisation-assessment).

### Who owns the technical migration?

Your platform or engineering team, unless you ask our [Software Development](/services/software-development) team to take it on. We own the customer side: what people see, keep and have to learn.

### We're already mid-migration. Is it too late?

No. We start from where the programme is and design the in-between states alongside the people moving the data.
