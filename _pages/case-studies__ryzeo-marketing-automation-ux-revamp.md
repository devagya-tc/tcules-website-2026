*Case study · Ryzeo*

# Reports ecommerce teams can use to decide what comes next

Ryzeo's ecommerce marketing-automation customers needed reporting that helped them understand performance and decide where to act, while navigation and inconsistent patterns also needed attention. Tcules combined heuristic review, stakeholder conversations, competitor analysis, user interviews and product analytics to prioritise the product changes, then developed information architecture, prototypes and reusable interface rules.

## At a glance {#case-context}

| Product | Ryzeo, marketing-automation and reporting software for ecommerce teams. |
|---|---|
| Our role | Heuristic review, stakeholder conversations, competitor analysis, user interviews, product analytics, information architecture, prototypes and a reusable component system. |
| The key question | Which changes would make reporting more useful first, without turning every known problem into one redesign? |
| What we delivered | A clearer reporting design and documented interface rules that carry the changes beyond a single report. |

Related Tcules services: [Product Redesign](/services/product-modernisation/product-redesign) and [Data Visualisation](/services/product-design/data-visualisation).

## Where to start on growth and retention {#the-starting-point-was-a-broad-product-situation}
Ryzeo wanted the product to contribute more to customer growth and retention. Tcules examined where the experience was making it harder for customers to understand value and use the product effectively.

Tcules examined the product from several angles:

- stakeholder conversations revealed business and roadmap concerns;
- competitor analysis exposed category expectations and alternatives;
- user interviews provided qualitative accounts of work and friction;
- product analytics showed where behaviour occurred; and
- heuristic review identified interaction and information problems that could be examined further.

No single input determined the priority. Together they helped the team decide where a redesign could create a coherent first step.

## Reports should help people decide what to do next {#reporting-was-one-decision-interface}

Reports are not only a display of historical data. In a marketing-automation product, they help a user decide what happened, what may have contributed, what requires attention and what action to take next. A customisable report can increase relevance, but it can also introduce configuration burden and conflicting definitions.

Customers wanted more useful insights from the reports. Tcules made that need a priority, working on how information was organised and how people could tailor the view to the questions they were asking.

## A shared system carried the change beyond one report {#a-shared-system-carried-the-change-beyond-one-re}

Tcules developed information architecture, mid- and high-fidelity wireframes, prototypes and a component-based design system with documentation. The system mattered because a reporting redesign could otherwise become another isolated interface inside the product.

Components and shared rules made it possible to express repeated filters, states, data treatments and navigation patterns consistently. Documentation connected the visual decisions to later product work.

## A clearer reporting experience, with rules the product could reuse {#what-the-case-proves-and-what-remains-unknown}

Ryzeo received a redesign direction that made reporting more useful and gave recurring interface decisions a shared home. Information architecture, prototypes and documented components connected the immediate work to future product development.

Tcules could improve a high-value part of the experience without leaving another isolated design for the next release to work around.

## Use this when a roadmap holds too many valid problems {#use-this-when-a-roadmap-holds-too-many-valid-pro}

The Ryzeo case is useful when a roadmap has more valid problems than one team can address coherently. The first [SaaS Product Redesign](/services/product-modernisation/product-redesign) target should be selected by consequence and dependency, not by the loudest request or the easiest screen.

A practical discussion begins with four questions:

1. Which user decision is currently weak?
2. What evidence supports that diagnosis?
3. Which other product areas depend on the same objects or interface rules?
4. What would need to be released and observed before the team could call the change useful?

## What the reporting work changed {#delivery-impact}

| Impact dimension | What changed |
|---|---|
| Prioritisation | Heuristic review, stakeholder input, competitor analysis, interviews and product analytics were used to decide which product problems deserved redesign attention first. |
| Decision usefulness | Reporting was treated as an interface for deciding what to do next, not only as a display of historical data. |
| Reuse | A shared component system gave recurring interface decisions a place to live beyond the first reporting redesign. |
