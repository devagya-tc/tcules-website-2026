# An AI conversation became a workspace for energy decisions

ConnectX was defining an AI-native building-energy product in which a user's intent needed to become a reviewable workspace rather than only a chat answer. Tcules turned personas, energy metrics, feature ideas and digital-twin concepts into eight scenarios, an adaptive interaction model and a coded demo, then took that demo through to a production-ready module, live since late 2025.

A request could now lead into a workspace with the relevant data, modules and assets together.

## ConnectX began with product material but no settled interaction model {#starting-point}

A facilities operator, finance leader and sustainability team can enter the same energy system with very different questions. One needs an exception that requires action. Another needs cost or forecast context. A third needs progress against an environmental target.
The early concepts contained much of the right material, but no dependable path from a person's request to the data, product state and action they needed. One dashboard for everyone would keep everything available by keeping too much visible. Separate fixed dashboards would multiply product structures and still fail whenever a situation crossed roles.
The starting material contained the ingredients of the product. The work was to give them a structure that could respond to a person’s purpose.

*ConnectX’s early product concepts.*

## Which product model could turn intent into a reviewable workspace? {#choice}

One direction kept a conventional dashboard and added an AI assistant. It protected familiarity but left the interface largely fixed. The other let conversational intent determine which KPIs, assets, charts and twin views appeared. It adapted more deeply, but a reply alone could not support continuing comparison and direct manipulation.
The selected model combined both strengths: conversation identifies the work; the system classifies that intent against a known situation; permitted tools retrieve data; the product assembles the relevant modules and twin state; the workspace remains available for ordinary interaction.
Chat did not replace the dashboard. It became one way to construct the right dashboard for the work at hand.

*Conversation creates the workspace; the modules give people a way to keep working with the result.*

## Eight scenarios made the adaptive behaviour testable {#turning-adaptation-into-scenarios}

An interface that can assemble anything is difficult to specify, evaluate or trust. Tcules and ConnectX defined eight representative situations across system status, forecasting, asset performance and operational conditions.
Each scenario named the initiating request, detected intent, required data, interface modules, twin behaviour and next available action. The model could adapt within those defined product rules.

### One request, a complete decision context {#one-request-a-complete-decision-context}

An operator asking about a peak-price event needs more than a suggested battery action. They need to see the affected assets, the data behind the suggestion and what the action would change.

The scenario work connected the request to that wider context: the information to display, the controls to offer and the state the workspace should preserve. Each scenario gave the adaptive interface a specific job to do.

## The digital twin had to support inspection as well as visualisation {#designing-the-digital-twin}

Early directions explored detailed 3D buildings, maps and realistic representation. Greater fidelity did not necessarily help someone understand the energy system.
The useful questions were selective: which assets are connected, where are they, what state are they in, and which one matters now? The chosen 2.5D direction treated the twin as an explanation layer. It could highlight, dim or bring an asset into focus according to the scenario.
This was not visual simplification for its own sake. It was a decision about the twin's job: explain the system rather than reproduce the building.

*The twin uses abstraction to expose connected assets and their relevance within the concept.*

## The demo ran in code, on sample data {#the-demonstration-ran-in-code-on-interim-data}

The coded demonstration made the product direction concrete enough to review as a whole. ConnectX could examine how a request, the relevant information and the twin belonged together, rather than judging separate dashboard concepts.

*The coded demo, shown with sample data.*

## Use chat to set up the work, and the workspace to do it {#chat-is-an-interaction-mode-not-a-complete-produ}

When people need to inspect, compare and act on changing information, decide what conversation controls and what remains visible as durable product state. Begin with a small set of consequential scenarios. For each, define the request, basis, permitted tools, interface state, action and recovery. That gives an AI system useful freedom without giving it accidental product authority.
