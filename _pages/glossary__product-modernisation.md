# Product modernisation

Software product modernisation updates an established application's workflows, interface and implementation while preserving the business behaviour and data that must survive. Application modernisation can include rehosting, replatforming, refactoring, replacing or retiring parts of the software; product work establishes how those changes affect the people using it.

When it matters: The term frames the decision when a product is too valuable to abandon and too expensive to keep changing. Somebody has already said “just rebuild it” out loud. What must survive is as much the question as what must change.

Product modernisation is broader than visual redesign and more product-specific than [platform migration](/services/product-modernisation/replatforming-product-design). A team might preserve deep links and saved work while replacing a broken status model, moving one complete operating path at a time. Capabilities may be preserved, repaired, redesigned, replatformed, rebuilt or retired.

[UX debt](/glossary/ux-debt) can explain why repeated workarounds make further change expensive. An [Application Modernisation Assessment](/audits/product-modernisation-assessment) helps choose the intervention.

## How is product modernisation used in practice? {#practice}

Product modernisation starts with the capability creating pressure rather than assuming the whole application needs to be rebuilt. The team establishes what behaviour must survive, what can change and which dependency is actually limiting the product. [Benchmark Gensuite](/case-studies/benchmark-gensuite-enterprise-suite-modernisation) used shared-component and coded-prototype work to review a modernisation direction before wider implementation.

The [Product Modernisation service](/services/product-modernisation) can combine research, redesign, Design Systems, Design Engineering and software delivery according to the product constraint.

## How does modernisation differ from redesign or replatforming? {#comparison}

The table separates the broader product decision from two possible interventions.

| Intervention | Main question | What may stay unchanged |
|---|---|---|
| Product modernisation | What should change, what should stay and how should the transition happen? | Any capability or foundation that still serves the product |
| Product redesign | How should an established workflow or interface change for existing and new users? | Viable technical foundations and unaffected product areas |
| Replatforming | How should product behaviour map to a new platform and transition path? | Behaviour that the target platform can support without harmful change |
