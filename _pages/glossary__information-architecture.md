*Glossary · Information Architecture*

# Information architecture

Information architecture defines the objects, relationships, labels and routes through which people find information and act in a product. When it matters: Use information architecture when people cannot predict where information belongs or how to find it. Investigate the record structure alongside the navigation, especially when the same work crosses screens, roles and spreadsheets.

In workflow software it extends beyond navigation. A staffing candidate, for example, can belong to pools, jobs and organisations. Those object relationships need to be clear before a team decides where links or screens should appear.

[Workflow design](/glossary/workflow-design) adds roles, rules, state and hand-offs. A sitemap is one expression of information architecture, not the whole discipline.

Explore [Information Architecture and Workflow Design](/services/product-design/information-architecture-workflow-design) or see the [Scissors staffing-product object model](/case-studies/scissors-staffing-software-object-oriented-ux).

## How is information architecture used in practice? {#practice}

Information architecture is useful when a product team cannot improve navigation without first clarifying what the product contains. In [Dealpath](/case-studies/enterprise-workflow-redesign-dealpath-real-estate), checking whether a listing already belonged to a deal changed the creation path without requiring the surrounding product to be reorganised. The structural decision came before the screen change.

Tcules' [Information Architecture and Workflow Design](/services/product-design/information-architecture-workflow-design) work combines records, relationships, roles, states and routes through the product.

## How does information architecture differ from navigation? {#comparison}

The table separates the structure of the product from one way a person moves through it.

| Information architecture | Navigation |
|---|---|
| Defines records, relationships, roles, states and meaningful groupings | Provides routes and wayfinding through that structure |
| Can reveal that the product model itself is unclear | Can be redesigned only after the underlying distinctions are understood |
| Exists beyond menus and page hierarchy | Is one visible expression of the architecture |

## Why information architecture matters in product work {#next}

Structure becomes a product decision once several roles need the same records to mean the same thing.
