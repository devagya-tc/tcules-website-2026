# Mobile apps, designed and built for SaaS products

An app is worth building when the task needs a phone: field work, patchy signal, the camera, location or a notification worth the interruption. Our designers work out what belongs on the small screen, and our developers build it with them.

[Start a conversation](#enquiry)

*Selected work: CuramCare · OctiFi*

## When your product needs an app {#format}

Plenty of SaaS products are well served by a web app that works on any screen. An app earns its place when one of these is true:

**The work happens away from a desk.** Carers between visits, engineers on site, a sales team in the car park.

**Signal comes and goes.** People need to keep working, and to trust that what they entered has saved.

**The phone does part of the job.** Taking photos, scanning, finding a location or signing on screen.

**Timing matters.** A notification has to reach the right person, and tapping it has to open the right screen.

**People open it many times a day.** Short, repeated tasks are quicker from a home screen than a browser tab.

Still unsure? Our guide, [Does your B2B SaaS product need a mobile app?](/insights/guides/saas-does-your-app-need-a-mobile-version), walks through the questions.

## What we design and build for the phone {#scope}

| Part of the app | What we work out with you |
|---|---|
| **Which tasks go mobile** | The few jobs that belong on a phone, instead of every web screen shrunk down |
| **Working offline** | What people can see, create and send without signal, and what happens when two changes clash |
| **Notifications** | Which ones are worth the interruption, and which screen each one opens |
| **Device features** | Camera, location, files and permissions, including what happens when someone taps "Don't allow" |
| **Release** | App store submission, updates, crash reports and support for people on older versions |

Designers and developers settle these together, because each one changes the screens and the code at once. The rule for syncing data after a dead zone shapes what a field engineer sees as much as any layout.

## Why SaaS teams build apps with us {#why-tcules}

**We ask whether you need one first.** If a web app will do the job, you will hear it from us before any budget goes on an app.

**Phone and desk stay one product.** The app uses the same records, rules and words as your web product, so customers never relearn anything when they switch.

**Designers and developers work together through the build.** When the code throws up a question, a designer answers it before anyone guesses.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Do you build for iOS and Android?

Yes. For most SaaS apps we recommend one cross-platform codebase that runs on both, so new features reach every customer together. A native build makes sense when the app leans heavily on the device, and we talk the trade-off through with you first.

### How does the app work without signal?

We agree which tasks must keep working offline, what the phone stores and what happens when someone else changed the same record in the meantime. The app then shows clearly what has saved and what is still waiting to send.

### Do you handle App Store and Google Play releases?

Yes. We prepare store listings and submissions, set up crash reporting and plan updates so customers on older versions keep working. Your team can take over releases, or we can look after the app through [Product Maintenance](/services/software-development/product-maintenance).

### Can you design the app and let our team build it?

Yes, as we did for OctiFi. Your engineers get designs for every state, prototypes they can try on a phone and written notes on offline use, notifications and permissions, so nothing is left to guesswork.
