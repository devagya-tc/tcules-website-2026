# Improve commerce by finding the broken evidence path

A commerce experience helps a person build enough confidence to move from discovery to commitment and then shows that the promise can be fulfilled. Conversion is one outcome of that path. It is not proof that any single interface pattern worked, and a higher rate can conceal returns, support cost or poor-fit orders.

By Yash Bhatt · Published 2 May 2025 · Updated 8 September 2026

## Five decisions shape the purchase {#five-decisions-shape-the-purchase}

### 1. Can I find a viable option? {#1-can-i-find-a-viable-option}

Navigation and search should reflect how people distinguish products. For a technical catalogue, material, compatibility and availability may matter more than lifestyle categories. For configurable products, the product may need to guide construction rather than retrieve a finished item.

### 2. Can I evaluate it? {#2-can-i-evaluate-it}

Product information should resolve uncertainty: dimensions, compatibility, condition, price basis, delivery, return constraints and evidence of quality. The correct hierarchy depends on the buying risk.

### 3. Can I commit knowingly? {#3-can-i-commit-knowingly}

Cart and checkout must preserve the chosen configuration and show material cost, timing and policy before commitment. Authentication, address, payment and review should not create avoidable re-entry or ambiguity.

### 4. Will the promise be fulfilled? {#4-will-the-promise-be-fulfilled}

Availability, production, delivery and status are part of the purchase decision. A fast checkout does not compensate for uncertain fulfilment.

### 5. Can I recover? {#5-can-i-recover}

People need to change, cancel, return, report or reorder. Recovery shapes trust before and after purchase.

## Each stage fails in its own visible way {#each-stage-fails-in-its-own-visible-way}

| Stage | Customer question | Product evidence | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Is there a relevant option? | Search model, filters, category meaning | Repeated reformulation, empty results |
| Evaluation | Will this meet my need? | Specification, fit, proof, total cost | Comparison loops, support questions |
| Commitment | Do I understand what I am agreeing to? | Order summary, policy, payment state | Checkout exit, duplicated payment |
| Fulfilment | Will it arrive as expected? | Availability, production and tracking state | Status contacts, late exceptions |
| Recovery | Can the problem be resolved? | Change, cancel, return and support path | Chargeback, repeated contact, abandonment |

[Commerce and Transactional Products](/expertise/commerce-transactional-products) applies this path to product structure, fulfilment and recovery.

The table is a diagnostic, not a claim that every signal has one cause.

## Commerce platforms are also operating systems {#commerce-platforms-are-also-operating-systems}

Many commerce projects involve staff, suppliers, warehouses, production or service teams. The customer surface depends on internal state. If “available” means something different to inventory, production and checkout, interface polish will not repair the promise.

The distinction is especially important for B2B, made-to-order and high-variation commerce. Product configuration, quote, order and fulfilment may form one connected workflow rather than a simple catalogue funnel.

## AI should reduce uncertainty, not invent certainty {#ai-should-reduce-uncertainty-not-invent-certaint}

Recommendation, search and assistance can help people discover and compare. Show why an item is relevant where that explanation changes the decision. Do not fabricate fit, availability or policy. Allow the person to refine the basis of a recommendation and recover from a poor one. [AI Search and Recommendation](/services/ai-product-ux/ai-search-recommendation) develops these choices into an explicit product interaction.

## Measure the whole path, not one conversion event {#measure-the-whole-path}

Use measures that can reveal trade-offs:

- successful searches and reformulation;
- product comparison and support questions;
- configuration completion and correction;
- checkout completion and payment recovery;
- fulfilment exceptions;
- returns and reasons;
- repeat purchase where it represents value;
- customer-support effort.

Tcules works on [commerce and transactional products](/expertise/commerce-transactional-products). The [Gold Image case](/case-studies/gold-image-printing-web-to-print-erp-modernisation) shows why customer and production workflows sometimes need one product model.

## Why did the customer abandon the path? {#why-did-the-customer-abandon-the-path}

The same abandonment event can represent different states. A person may be blocked by a form or payment failure, uncertain about landed cost or returns, or still comparing options without an intention to buy now. Segment the path before choosing a remedy; recovery messaging cannot repair missing evidence, and a shorter checkout cannot resolve product-fit doubt.

Show total cost, delivery constraints, returns and warranty evidence at the point each becomes decision-relevant. Also make non-product information findable through search and navigation. Treat guest checkout as the default unless an account is necessary for the purchased service and that dependency is explained before commitment.
