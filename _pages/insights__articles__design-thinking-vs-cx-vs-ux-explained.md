# UX vs CX vs design thinking: the difference, and when to use each

By [Yash Bhatt](/insights/authors/yash-bhatt) · Published 2 May 2025 · Updated 26 October 2026 · 7 min read

This article on UX vs CX vs design thinking explains what each one covers. It shows where the boundary between them falls in a B2B product. It gives you three tests for choosing the right one for your problem.

UX describes a person's experience of using or anticipating a product or service. Customer experience covers the wider relationship across discovery, purchase, use, support, renewal and exit. Design thinking describes approaches for investigating uncertain problems and developing possible responses. The terms overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

| Term | What it scopes | Example decision | Common misuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| UX | A person's experience of using or anticipating a product or service | Can an operator understand and recover a failed workflow? | Reduced to interface appearance |
| CX | The wider relationship across channels and time | Does the sales promise survive onboarding, support and renewal? | Treated as one linear map |
| Design thinking | An approach to framing, learning and developing responses | Which problem deserves testing before a solution is committed? | Presented as a fixed workshop sequence |

[ISO 9241-210](https://www.iso.org/standard/77520.html) situates user experience within human-centred design for interactive systems. Organisations may use “CX” and “design thinking” differently, so names alone do not allocate responsibility.

**Key takeaways**

- UX covers a person's experience of a product, customer experience covers the wider relationship, and design thinking is an approach to framing uncertain problems.
- No one term owns the customer. Name the decision being made before choosing which one to apply.
- If a task fails inside the product, begin with UX. If several functions together cause the failure, the boundary is customer experience.
- One initiative can use all three, provided each activity states the decision it informs, the evidence it needs and its owner.

## CX vs UX examples: where the boundary falls in a B2B product {#where-the-boundary-between-them-actually-falls}

Suppose a B2B product's sales team promises fast approval. In practice the customer must configure roles, migrate data and wait for support before the first request can run. Who owns that gap?

Each term covers a different part of it:

- UX work can improve configuration, permissions, request status and recovery.
- CX work can align sales, onboarding, support and renewal expectations.
- Human-centred design methods can help the organisation understand why the promise and the operating reality diverged.

[Product Design](/services/product-design) can own the product experience and its path into implementation when the issue is narrower than the whole customer relationship.

No one term owns the customer. The useful questions are which decision is being made, whose experience is affected, which evidence is available and who can change the underlying system.

## Design thinking vs UX: is design thinking a process? {#design-thinking-vs-ux}

Design thinking is an approach for framing problems that are still uncertain and testing possible responses. It is often taught as a short run of stages, such as the Stanford d.school's empathise, define, ideate, prototype and test. In practice it works best as a loop of methods, chosen to suit the problem in front of the team.

UX design is a discipline with a responsibility: how a product works for the people who use it. UX designers borrow design-thinking methods all the time. Anyone else in a company can use them too, which is why the two are easy to confuse.

| | Design thinking | UX design |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A way of working through uncertain problems | A discipline responsible for the product experience |
| Who uses it | Anyone: product, operations, leadership, design | Designers and researchers, with product and engineering |
| What it produces | A framed problem, tested options, a direction | Flows, interfaces and research that shape the shipped product |

## UX vs CX vs service design {#ux-vs-cx-vs-service-design}

Service design is the term that most often joins this comparison. It works on the whole service, including the people, processes and systems behind the scenes that deliver it, and teams often map it as a service blueprint.

The three fit together. CX describes how the whole relationship feels to the customer over time. Service design changes the front and back of the service that produce that feeling. UX covers the product moments inside it. A CX team frequently uses service design as its method, and the product changes it calls for land with UX and product teams.

## Who should own UX and CX? Start from the choice being made {#put-the-decision-before-the-discipline-label}

Name the decision first. [User journey mapping](/glossary/user-journey-mapping) connects evidence across time and touchpoints. Scope it to the decision it informs, because it cannot stand in for the whole customer experience.

Product use sits inside a wider customer relationship that crosses channels and organisational processes. Design approaches can work across both.

When the break sits inside the product, that is [Product Design](/services/product-design) work, done with your product and engineering teams.

## Three tests for choosing UX, CX or design thinking {#which-boundary-is-large-enough-to-explain-the-fa}

Start with the observed break.

- If a person cannot understand or complete a task inside the product, begin with UX.
- If sales, onboarding, support, policy and product together create the failure, the boundary is customer experience.
- If the team does not yet know which problem or opportunity is worth pursuing, design-thinking methods can frame and test the possibilities.

One initiative can use all three. Each activity must state the decision it informs, the evidence it needs and the owner who can act on the result.

Then ask who sits at a level that can change the combined system. Ask too who reports into that decision from product, operations, policy and customer communication. If no one has that authority, the first decision is about escalation and governance. More research or design activity is unlikely to resolve it.

## Where UX met CX on Gold Image Printing's orders {#where-ux-met-cx-gold-image-printing}

Our work for [Gold Image Printing](/case-studies/gold-image-printing-web-to-print-erp-modernisation) sat right on this boundary. Its online orders continued into artwork, production instructions, scheduling, inventory, fulfilment and customer communication, across a commerce site and an internal ERP.

A screen review would have found inconsistent pages. So Tcules observed people using the existing system and examined the order process with stakeholders and analytics. That showed the dependency. A change that simplified customer ordering could still confuse production staff, and an ERP change could make order status harder to read elsewhere.

The customer's experience depended on the whole order, which is CX territory. The fix still ran through product design: one set of components and one style guide across the store and the ERP, so both sides could change together. Naming that boundary shows which screens have to change as a pair.

## UX vs CX FAQs {#faq}

### What is the difference between UX and CX?

UX covers a person's experience of using a product or service. CX covers the whole relationship with the company, across discovery, purchase, onboarding, support, renewal and exit. A confusing settings screen is a UX problem; a sales promise that onboarding cannot keep is a CX problem.

### Is design thinking the same as UX design?

No. Design thinking is an approach for framing uncertain problems and testing responses, and anyone in a company can use it. UX design is the discipline responsible for how a product works for its users, and it often borrows design-thinking methods to get there.

### Who should own UX and who should own CX?

Product and design teams usually own UX, because they can change the product. CX needs someone senior enough to align sales, onboarding, support and product, since no single team controls the whole relationship. Where nobody holds that authority, agreeing who escalates comes before more research.

### How does service design relate to UX and CX?

Service design works on the whole service, including the people, processes and systems behind the scenes. CX describes how that service feels to the customer over time, and UX covers the product moments within it. Service design is often the method a CX team uses to change what customers experience.

### Is design thinking a process?

It is usually taught as one, with stages such as empathise, define, ideate, prototype and test. In practice it works as a loop of methods for framing and testing, chosen to suit the problem. Running it as a fixed workshop sequence is a common misuse.
