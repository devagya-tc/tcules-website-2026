# UX, CX and design thinking name different things

UX describes a person’s experience of using or anticipating a product or service. Customer experience covers the wider relationship across discovery, purchase, use, support, renewal and exit. Design thinking describes approaches for investigating uncertain problems and developing possible responses. The terms overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

By Yash Bhatt · Published 2 May 2025 · Updated 8 September 2026

| Term | What it scopes | Example decision | Common misuse |

|---|---|---|---|

| UX | A person's experience of using or anticipating a product or service | Can an operator understand and recover a failed workflow? | Reduced to interface appearance |

| CX | The wider relationship across channels and time | Does the sales promise survive onboarding, support and renewal? | Treated as one linear map |

| Design thinking | An approach to framing, learning and developing responses | Which problem deserves testing before a solution is committed? | Presented as a fixed workshop sequence |

[ISO 9241-210](https://www.iso.org/standard/77520.html) situates user experience within human-centred design for interactive systems. Organisations may use “CX” and “design thinking” differently, so names alone do not allocate responsibility.

## Where the boundary between them actually falls {#where-the-boundary-between-them-actually-falls}

Consider a B2B product whose sales team promises fast approval, but the customer must configure roles, migrate data and wait for support before the first request can run.

[Product Design](/services/product-design) can own the product experience and its implementation path when the issue is narrower than the complete customer relationship.

- UX work may improve configuration, permissions, request state and recovery.
- CX work may align sales, onboarding, support and renewal expectations.
- Human-centred design methods may help the organisation understand why the promise and operating reality diverged.

No one term owns the customer. The useful questions are which decision is being made, whose experience is affected, which evidence is available and who can change the underlying system.

## Put the decision before the discipline label {#put-the-decision-before-the-discipline-label}

[User journey mapping](/glossary/user-journey-mapping) can connect evidence across time and touchpoints, but it should follow the scope of the decision rather than stand in for the whole customer experience.

Product use sits inside a wider customer relationship that may cross channels and organisational processes. Design approaches can work across both. The practical scope comes from the decision, the available evidence and the owner who can change the underlying system. Treating UX, CX or design thinking as competing territories does not establish it.

Tcules uses [Product Design](/services/product-design) for product decisions and carries relevant findings into [How We Work](/how-we-work) with client product and engineering teams.

## Which boundary is large enough to explain the failure? {#which-boundary-is-large-enough-to-explain-the-fa}

Start with the observed break. If a person cannot understand or complete a task inside the product, begin with UX. If the failure is created across sales, onboarding, support, policy and product, the relevant boundary is customer experience. If the team does not yet know which problem or opportunity is worth pursuing, design-thinking methods may help frame and test possibilities.

UX, CX and design thinking are not competing departments. One initiative can use all three, but each activity must state the decision it informs, the evidence it needs and the owner who can act on the result.

The useful question is not which discipline owns the customer. It is which single person sits at a level that can change the combined system, and who reports into that decision from product, operations, policy and customer communication. If no one has that authority, the first decision concerns escalation and governance. Another research or design activity cannot resolve it.
