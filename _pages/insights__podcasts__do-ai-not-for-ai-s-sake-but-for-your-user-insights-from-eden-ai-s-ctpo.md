# Build AI for the user's work, not for AI's sake

In this Tcules conversation, Jatin Leuva, Founder and CEO of Tcules, speaks with Samy Melaine, Founder and CPTO of Eden AI, about building a developer platform in a fast-changing AI market. This article paraphrases the published recording rather than presenting a verbatim transcript.

By Jatin Leuva · Published 6 April 2026 · Updated 8 September 2026

[Watch the published recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QNz1zr3U7Zo).

## Model choice was a product problem before it was technical {#model-choice-was-a-product-problem-before-it-was}

Eden AI was created around a market condition: developers could access a growing number of AI models and providers, but comparing, integrating and operating them imposed repeated work. Samy describes a platform that brings provider choice, model orchestration and production observability into one developer environment.

A multi-model product model matters because choice alone can increase cognitive and operational load. The user needs to understand which model is appropriate, connect models into a workflow, test the behaviour and observe what happens in production.

## Technical and product decisions belong in the same conversation {#technical-and-product-decisions-belong-in-the-sa}

Samy's CPTO role combines technical and product responsibility. In the recording, he explains that a developer tool must be dependable while also making unfamiliar AI capability understandable. He does not describe design as a layer applied after the technology. The team moves between user feedback, technical feasibility and usability.

One operating sequence emerges from the conversation:

1. A user problem or feedback enters the team.
2. Developers examine the product and technical possibilities.
3. Product and design shape the proposal into an understandable interaction.
4. The proposal returns to engineering for challenge and implementation.
5. Product use creates the next evidence.

Samy also notes that developers contribute product insight because they use the developer-facing platform and participate in support. That is a specific organisational condition, not a rule that engineers automatically know the user better.

## The roadmap is a communication tool {#the-roadmap-is-a-communication-tool}

The conversation resists the idea that a roadmap can predict a fast-moving AI market for six months with false confidence. A roadmap still has value when it communicates current direction, constraints and confidence rather than pretending to be a fixed inventory of future features.

For AI products, this implies that roadmap items should carry:

- the user problem;
- current capability and uncertainty;
- technical dependency;
- confidence in the proposed product response;
- evidence required before wider commitment.

## A wider audience changes what control has to look like {#a-wider-audience-changes-what-control-has-to-loo}

An API-first product can begin with technical reliability. As it serves a wider range of developers, including people with less AI-specific experience, model selection and workflow construction become product-experience problems.

The task is not to hide every technical distinction. It is to translate the distinctions that matter and keep a route to operational detail. A non-specialist may need a plain explanation of model fit; an experienced developer may need latency, provider, cost, observability and failure information.

## What the recording supports, and where it stops {#what-the-recording-supports-and-where-it-stops}

| Theme in the recording | Product decision it informs | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Many providers and models | Create a comparison and orchestration model | Do not imply that every model is interchangeable |
| API-first reliability | Protect production stability | Reliability does not remove usability work |
| Developer participation in support | Bring implementation knowledge into product discovery | Product insight still needs user evidence |
| Roadmap as communication | Record direction and confidence | Avoid fixed certainty in a changing market |
| User problem before AI feature | Start from work and value | Technical exploration may still reveal new possibilities |

## Find the work worth improving before scoping the AI {#find-the-work-worth-improving-before-scoping-the}

Building AI for the user's work is not a case against technical ambition. It is a sequence: find the work worth improving, understand what the technology makes possible, define the product boundary and learn from use. Technical and design judgement move together.

The related [Eden AI case](/case-studies/eden-ai-workflow-builder-search-redesign) describes Tcules' bounded design contribution to workflow creation and search. [AI Products and Developer Tools](/expertise/ai-products-developer-tools) explains the wider product situations.

## How to use this episode {#how-to-use-this-episode}

Use the conversation as a record of the guest's product reasoning, not as universal implementation guidance. The transcript-backed material explains why user work should precede model novelty and why product and technical roadmaps must be discussed together. The control questions around authority, recovery and disclosure are our editorial addition, not Samy's. They follow the same reasoning as [calibrated trust in AI interfaces](/insights/articles/building-trust-and-transparency-in-ai-first-user-interfaces), where the external sources are cited.

After listening, name one user decision, the minimum model role that supports it and the recovery path when the model cannot help. If those cannot be stated, the roadmap item remains a capability label rather than a product commitment.

## What authority and recovery controls does an AI workflow need? {#what-authority-and-recovery-controls-does-an-ai-}

Enforce authority at each tool or action boundary, not only in a prompt or top-level workflow label. Before execution, show the affected objects, relevant evidence, permission, consequence and review state. After execution, preserve a visible result, partial-failure state and a route to correct, retry, stop or hand work back to a person. The right control depends on consequence and reversibility; the recording supports the product context, while this control model is current Tcules editorial guidance rather than a claim attributed to the guest.
