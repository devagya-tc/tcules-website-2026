*Software Development · Product maintenance*

# Maintenance and new features for your live SaaS product

Fixes, dependency and security updates, performance work and new features, done in an order you can see and change, with your product's rules and history written down as we go.

[Start a conversation](#enquiry)

## Signs your product needs steady care {#recognition}

**Small fixes take weeks.** Every change starts with someone rediscovering how that part of the code works.

**Upgrades keep losing to features.** Frameworks and libraries fall behind until one routine update becomes a project of its own.

**The same bug keeps coming back.** Support closes the ticket, and the cause stays in the code.

**Releases make everyone nervous.** Changes wait because nobody is sure what else they might break.

## Five kinds of change, each with its own finish line {#change-types}

| Kind of change | What it covers | How we know it's done |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixes** | Behaviour that is wrong | The agreed behaviour is back, with a test to keep it there |
| **Updates** | Frameworks, libraries, APIs and security patches | Everything users rely on still works the same way |
| **Reliability and speed** | Slow pages and repeat failures | The problem is measured before the change and checked after it |
| **Improvements** | A better version of an existing workflow | The task gets easier and nothing else breaks |
| **New features** | Something the product has never done | Planned and designed as its own piece of work |

A steady stream of urgent fixes is a signal in itself. When patches keep piling up, the product may need [modernising](/services/product-modernisation) instead.

## What gets done first {#priorities}

The oldest ticket is rarely the most important one. We weigh how many customers a problem touches, how often it recurs and how urgent any security risk is. Then we weigh the effort, and whether a quick patch stores up a bigger problem.

You see the order and the reasons, and you can change them.

## Knowledge that stays with the product {#knowledge}

We write down the domain rules, integrations, past incidents and the reasons behind key choices, in your repository or wiki. New people get up to speed from it, and so does your own team if you take the work back in-house.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### What does product maintenance include?

Fixes, dependency and security updates, performance and reliability work, small improvements and release management. We agree at the start which of these we own, so nothing important falls between teams.

### Can maintenance include new features?

Yes. Small improvements fit into the regular flow of work. Larger features are planned and designed as their own piece of work, so they get proper thought instead of being squeezed in as tickets.

### How is maintenance different from warranty support?

Warranty support fixes delivered work that falls short of what was agreed at release. Maintenance is the ongoing care and change that every live product needs after that.
