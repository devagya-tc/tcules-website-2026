# Marketplace product design for buyers, providers and the team in between

Buyers want confidence, providers want the right demand, and your operations team needs to see enough to step in. We design the exchange they all share, as we did for CuramCare's platform connecting families and carers.

[Start a conversation](#enquiry)

*Selected work: CuramCare*

## What each side needs from the same booking {#hard-parts}

Every marketplace turns on one thing changing hands: a booking, an order, a job or a lead. Each side looks at it with a different worry.

| Who | Before they commit | Once it's agreed |
|---|---|---|
| The buyer | "Can I trust this provider, and what exactly am I agreeing to?" | "Is it confirmed, and what happens if it goes wrong?" |
| The provider | "Is this request worth my time?" | "What changed, and when do I get paid?" |
| Your operations team | "Which matches look wrong?" | "Who is stuck, and what can I change for them?" |

Give each side its own app joined only by notifications, and your operations team ends up doing the joining by hand.

## How we design marketplaces {#approach}

**We start with the thing that changes hands.** Before drawing screens, we pin down the booking, order or job: what it holds, when it becomes binding, what can still change and who acts next.

**One booking, three angles.** Buyer, provider and operations each see their own part of it, so a change made by one shows up correctly for the others.

**Trust comes from things people can check.** Ratings and badges help. Clear availability, real prices, response times and plain cancellation terms help more, shown at the moment someone chooses.

**Every commitment comes with a way back.** Cancelling, changing, refunding and disputing are designed as stages of the booking, with rules both sides can read. Problems stay in the product instead of turning into email threads.

**Your operations team gets proper tools.** Exceptions, quality checks and payment problems need screens of their own, with a record of who changed what. An admin panel full of one-off fixes usually means the main product is missing something.

## Research with both sides of a care marketplace {#proof}

### CuramCare: families and self-employed carers on one platform

Families across the UK use CuramCare to find carers who will look after someone at home. The carers are self-employed. Clients and carers each have their own app, and a web platform sits between them.

Our work started with surveys of both groups about what got in their way. Their answers became a product plan, and the plan shaped our redesign of all three: web platform, client app and carer app. Our developers then coded the frontend.

## Questions from marketplace teams {#faq}

### How is marketplace design different from ecommerce design?

In ecommerce, one business controls the offer, the price and the delivery. A marketplace coordinates independent providers who set some of their own terms, so trust, matching and disputes become product features. For single-seller stores, see [Commerce & Transactional Products](/expertise/commerce-transactional-products).

### Do buyers and providers need separate apps?

Often, because their tasks differ. CuramCare has one app for clients and one for carers. What matters is that both read the same booking. Operations teams usually work best in a web tool of their own.

### What does our operations team need from the product?

A view of every exchange and its history, a list of the exceptions that need a person, and the authority to change things with a record of why. If your team fixes problems in spreadsheets or a database console today, we start there.

### Can AI help with matching?

Yes, provided people can see why a match was suggested and correct it when it is wrong. Our designers shape recommendations that show what they are based on. More in [AI search and recommendations](/services/ai-product-ux/ai-search-recommendation).

### Do you build marketplaces as well as design them?

Yes. For CuramCare, our developers built the frontend of the web platform and both apps from our designs. Our [Software Development](/services/software-development) team also takes on backend work, integrations and payments.
