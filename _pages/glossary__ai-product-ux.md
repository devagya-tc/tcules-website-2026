# AI Product UX

AI Product UX is the design of how people understand, direct, evaluate, control and recover from probabilistic model behaviour inside a product. When it matters: The term becomes practical the moment model output influences a real decision, record or commitment.

When model output affects a real decision, evidence, authority and recovery need owners. Interface polish alone cannot supply them.

AI Product UX covers user work, model role, sources, uncertainty, permissions, evaluation and recovery. It is broader than adding a chat interface and narrower than the whole AI engineering stack. [Agentic UX](/glossary/agentic-ux) is relevant when the system plans or acts across steps; a [human-in-the-loop workflow](/glossary/human-in-the-loop) defines where a person can meaningfully intervene.

For example, an AI recommendation needs a visible objective, basis, alternatives and decision owner. An AI action additionally needs permission, execution state and recovery. BuildTwin shows one bounded pattern for [expert review of AI quality-control output](/case-studies/ai-quality-control-expert-review-buildtwin).

Read about [calibrated trust in AI interfaces](/insights/articles/building-trust-and-transparency-in-ai-first-user-interfaces) or explore the [AI Product UX service](/services/ai-product-ux).

## How is AI Product UX used in practice? {#practice}

AI Product UX starts with the work a person is trying to complete and the role AI is allowed to play in it. In [Omny AI](/case-studies/omny-ai-suggestion-trust-redesign), Tcules kept AI suggestions separate from the seller's decision until the person accepted them. In [BuildTwin](/case-studies/ai-quality-control-expert-review-buildtwin), the review model kept engineering judgement at the centre of AI quality control.

The BuildTwin, Omny AI and ConnectX examples involve different interfaces, but the same product questions recur: what the model may do, what evidence a person sees, who can override it and how the product recovers when the output is wrong.

## How does AI Product UX differ from adding an AI feature? {#comparison}

The table distinguishes a model capability from the product behaviour required around it.

| Focus | Question | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| AI capability | What can the model or service produce? | Model output, technical evaluation and integration behaviour |
| AI Product UX | How should a person use, verify, control or reject that capability in real work? | Scenarios, interaction states, source/evidence display, authority rules and recovery paths |

## Why ai product ux matters in product work {#next}

The discipline covers what the model contributes, what people can inspect, and how recovery works when the output is wrong.
