*Prototyping & usability testing*

# Change your mind in a prototype. It costs less there.

From rough clickable flows to working code, we build only as much prototype as the question needs, then test it with people who match your users. Each round ends with what to change.

[Start a conversation](#enquiry)

*Selected work: OctiFi · Auxentios*

## Questions a prototype can answer {#recognition}

**"Will people understand the new workflow?"** Watch them try it before it reaches the roadmap.

**"Which of these two directions should we build?"** Put both in front of users on the same task.

**"Does it still work with real data?"** Thousands of rows, missing values and very long text change everything.

**"Can the browser actually do this?"** Drag and drop, live updates and small screens are worth checking in code.

## The right prototype for the question {#fidelity}

| What you need to learn | The prototype that answers it |
|---|---|
| Does the order of steps make sense? | A rough clickable flow |
| Can people read and compare the information? | Realistic content and data, in high fidelity |
| Does the interaction feel right? | A detailed prototype with feedback and states |
| Will it work in a real browser? | A working prototype in code |

We add realism only where the question needs it. Anything extra just makes the prototype harder to change.

## How we run usability tests {#testing}

We find people who match your users, give them a realistic task and watch where the product helps or gets in the way. What people do counts for more than what they say they like.

After each round you get the tasks, who took part, what we saw, what we recommend changing and what still needs testing. Then we revise and go again with your team's input.

## Tested with users before the build {#proof}

### OctiFi: rough flows first, realistic prototypes next

For OctiFi's mobile lending app, we worked through the flows in rough form first, then made detailed prototypes filled with realistic data. Product, engineering and marketing reviewed the intended experience before OctiFi invested in code. The pilot grew into a longer engagement covering onboarding, loan issuance and new features.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### What should we prototype first?

The assumption that would cost most if it turns out wrong. That might be a core workflow, an unfamiliar interaction, dense data or a browser behaviour nobody has tried yet.

### How many people do you test with?

We keep testing each group of users until the same problems start repeating, then stop. We plan each round around the question and the groups that matter, and run another round when the first raises new questions.

### Who takes part in the tests?

People who match your users: existing customers, trial users or others who fit the profile. We agree that profile with you before the first session, and your team can watch the sessions.

### Can you test a prototype we built with AI tools?

Yes, like any other prototype. If you plan to put it in front of paying customers, a [Prototype Readiness Review](/audits/prototype-readiness-review) shows what needs rebuilding first.
