*Expertise · content and knowledge platforms*

# Six versions of the same policy. Which one counts?

Knowledge platform design for search, sources and AI answers: owner, version and date shown where people choose, so they can pick the right material and take it into their work.

[Start a conversation](#enquiry)

*Selected work: Eden AI · GT Tools · BuildTwin*

## Why people give up on search {#hard-parts}

**"Search returns six versions of the same policy."** Nobody can tell which one is current or who approved it.

**"Nobody owns half the articles."** Pages go stale, and readers cannot tell which ones anyone still checks.

**"Experts know the exact name. Newcomers can only describe the problem."** One search box has to serve both.

## How we design discovery {#approach}

| What people need to do | What we design |
|---|---|
| Find it | Search, filters and browsing for people who know the name and people who only know the problem |
| Judge it | Source, owner, version and date, shown where they help someone pick the right result |
| Check it | AI answers that link to the passages and documents behind them |
| Use it | A route from the result into the next task: add, share, cite or configure |

Search quality is partly the engine and partly the screen. Labels, previews, filters and empty results do more work than most teams expect.

The projects below show how we handle search results, sources and large catalogues.

### Eden AI: documentation beside the search result

Eden AI's developer platform holds templates, AI features and the documentation that explains them, and that documentation could drift away from the task in hand.

We designed its search results as preview cards: enough to judge a capability at a glance, with deeper technical detail a step further in and a direct link to its documentation.

[Read the Eden AI case](/case-studies/eden-ai-workflow-builder-search-redesign)

### GT Tools: list pages with enough detail to choose from

GT Tools sells specialist equipment for automotive-glass repair, and its buyers often have to confirm compatibility before choosing. In our redesign, category and product-list pages carry the details that narrow a choice, so comparing can start on the list itself. We designed the store and developed it on Shopify.

[Read the GT Tools case](/case-studies/gt-tools-shopify-product-discovery)

### BuildTwin: sources kept in the reading path

BuildTwin was adding AI checks to structural-engineering drawings, and an engineer had to review every result. Our first concept tucked the evidence into accordions, which meant click after click. The chosen design is a continuous feed: each observation sits with its source, status and conclusion, while search and filters still lead to any single check.

[Read the BuildTwin case](/case-studies/ai-quality-control-expert-review-buildtwin)

## Why platform teams work with us {#why-tcules}

**We design how results are judged as well as found.** Versions, owners and sources get a place in the interface, so people can tell which result to trust before they open it.

**We treat AI answers as something to check.** Our [AI Search & Recommendation](/services/ai-product-ux/ai-search-recommendation) service designs answers that show where they came from and what to do when they are wrong.

## Questions about search and discovery {#faq}

### Can you improve search without replacing our search engine?

Yes. A lot of search trouble sits in the interface: how the search box suggests and corrects queries, how results are labelled, filtered and previewed, and what happens when nothing matches. Ranking and indexing stay with your engineers.

### How should AI answers show their sources?

Close to the answer, with a way to open the exact passage or document. People should be able to tell a quoted source from the AI's own summary.

### How do you show which version of a document is current?

With the smallest set of signals that settles the question, usually status, owner and date. Older versions stay available without competing for attention.
