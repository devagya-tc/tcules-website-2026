*UX & Product Audit*

# A SaaS UX audit that finds why customers get stuck

For established SaaS and complex software. We review the product, walk through real tasks and check your analytics and support history, then hand you findings ranked by the harm they cause.

[Start a conversation](#enquiry)

*Selected work: Dealpath · Scissors*

## What usually brings teams here {#recognition}

**"Customers get stuck, and everyone has a different theory."** Support blames onboarding, sales blames missing features, engineering blames the design.

**"Sign-ups look healthy. Then people stall."** New users arrive, and the first important task goes unfinished.

**"The workflow has grown around its exceptions."** Roles, approvals and special cases have piled onto a journey built for something simpler.

**"Someone has already proposed a redesign."** You want to know what it has to fix before you pay for it.

## What we look at {#coverage}

| Where we look | What we want to find out |
|---|---|
| Tasks and workflows | Where people stall, loop back or give up |
| How the product is organised | Whether people can find things and see how records relate |
| Roles and permissions | Whether each role sees what it needs, and only that |
| Screens and controls | Whether labels, states, errors and next steps make sense |
| What you already know | What analytics, support tickets and past research confirm or contradict |

We start with an expert review and task walkthroughs of the live product, adding user interviews when your data runs out. A usability checklist can tell you a button is hard to see. It can't tell you why people press the wrong one.

## What you receive {#deliverables}

A ranked list of findings your team can act on. Each one sets out:

- **What happens:** what we saw, and where.
- **Who it affects:** the role or task.
- **Why it matters:** the cost to users or the business.
- **How sure we are:** seen in use, backed by data, or a hunch to test.
- **What to do next:** fix, redesign, test first or leave alone.

Findings are ranked by the harm they cause. A quiet flaw in how records connect can outrank an ugly screen. We agree the scope and the outputs with you before we start.

## Where we found the real problem before redesigning {#proof}

<!-- awaiting client sign-off -->
> “Tcules told us what was really wrong, including the things we didn't want to hear, and then fixed them with us.”  
> **Akshay Vyas, Creative Head, Knovos**

Both projects started with this kind of review, and the findings set what we redesigned.

**Dealpath.** We began with a product audit of Dealpath's real estate investment software, then narrowed in on its listing workflow. Of the four actions on a listing, only one had a structural fault: "Add to pipeline" went straight to creating a new deal, even for a property the team was already tracking.

We redesigned that path so people search for an existing deal first, with a new deal as the fallback. The comparable and pass actions stayed largely as users knew them. [Read the Dealpath case](/case-studies/enterprise-workflow-redesign-dealpath-real-estate)

**Scissors.** This staffing software was too new for its analytics to tell the whole story. Reviewing the product and talking to the people who would use it showed that clients, candidates and jobs needed to link to each other, so details entered once carried through. That finding shaped four redesigned workflows. [Read the Scissors case](/case-studies/scissors-staffing-software-object-oriented-ux)

## When an audit is worth it {#when}

When you know something is wrong but can't yet say what. One stubborn workflow, or onboarding on its own, is enough for a complete audit.

If the bigger question is whether to redesign, replatform or rebuild, start with a [Product Modernisation Assessment](/audits/product-modernisation-assessment). If the design is right and the live product doesn't match it, a [Design-to-Code Audit](/audits/design-to-code-audit) fits better.

## What happens after the audit {#next}

Your team can work from the findings alone. Or we can carry them into [Product Design](/services/product-design): redesigning the flagged workflows, testing the changes with users and handing over designs ready to build.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Is a UX audit the same as a heuristic evaluation?

A heuristic evaluation is one part of it. We also look at how the product is organised, how roles differ and what user behaviour shows, so each finding explains a cause as well as a symptom.

### Can you audit just one workflow, or only onboarding?

Yes. One important journey, such as onboarding or an approval flow, is often a better start than a light pass over everything.

### Do you interview users?

Yes, when the question needs it. Your analytics, support tickets and past research often answer part of it first.
