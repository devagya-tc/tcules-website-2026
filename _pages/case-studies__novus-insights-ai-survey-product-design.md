*Case study · Novus Insights*

# AI-assisted surveys with the researcher in charge

Novus Insights was exploring an AI-assisted product for creating questionnaires without handing research judgement to the model. Tcules combined secondary and competitor research, stakeholder interviews, personas and product definition to shape an MVP where AI could generate a starting questionnaire while the researcher remained responsible for purpose, wording, sequence and revision.

Our work covered secondary and competitor research, stakeholder interviews, personas for brand-management roles, workflow mapping, information design, proof-of-concept work and MVP design for prompt-led generation, question suggestions and editing.

## At a glance {#case-context}

| Product | Novus Insights' AI-assisted survey product, for brand managers and researchers writing questionnaires. |
|---|---|
| Our role | Secondary and competitor research, stakeholder interviews, personas, product definition and MVP design. |
| The key question | How can AI draft a useful first questionnaire while the researcher keeps control of its purpose, wording and order? |
| What we delivered | An MVP design in which AI suggestions stay editable and the researcher stays in charge of the final questionnaire. |

Related Tcules services: [Human-in-the-Loop Workflows](/services/ai-product-ux/human-in-the-loop-workflows) and [Product Design](/services/product-design).

## What did the survey product need to know before generating questions? {#the-product-needed-to-begin-with-intent}
The user was not asking the system to produce arbitrary text. A brand manager or researcher came with a decision they needed evidence for. The product direction therefore had to connect an initial prompt to a questionnaire structure that could be inspected and changed.

Tcules investigated the market and competitor context, spoke with stakeholders and developed role and workflow models. That work informed the information design for an MVP in which the system could generate a starting questionnaire and propose additional questions.

The important boundary was editability. A generated question was not automatically valid, unbiased or suitable for the audience. It remained material for a researcher to review.

## Suggestion was useful because it remained revisable {#suggestion-was-useful-because-it-remained-revisa}

The MVP put the generated draft and the researcher's edits in the same list, with each generated question carrying its source prompt. A question the researcher rewrote kept the original visible beneath it, so a reviewer could see what the model had proposed and what a person had changed. Those capabilities addressed different moments. Generation could create a first structure. Suggestions could help the researcher notice missing areas. Editing let the person correct assumptions, change order and align the instrument with the research purpose.

The researcher could use the model to get started, then shape the questionnaire around the actual study. Review and editing were part of the core workflow.

## From a research question to a questionnaire people can shape {#what-this-case-establishes}

Novus gained an MVP direction that connected research intent to a generated draft, further suggestions and deliberate editing. The product could accelerate the start of a questionnaire while keeping the researcher responsible for its wording and purpose.

## Use this when an expert must stand behind AI output {#use-this-when-an-expert-must-stand-behind-ai-out}

The pattern applies when AI generates material that an expert must stand behind. The product should distinguish:

- the user's underlying decision;
- the system's proposed output;
- the criteria used to review it;
- the changes made by the expert; and
- the state that counts as approved.

Without those distinctions, faster generation can increase the amount of unexamined work. With them, the model can provide leverage while the product preserves professional responsibility.

The interaction pattern in this case is what Tcules designs as [human-in-the-loop workflows](/services/ai-product-ux/human-in-the-loop-workflows): the AI proposes, an accountable expert reviews, and the product records the difference.

## What AI could help draft without owning the study {#delivery-impact}

| Impact dimension | What changed |
|---|---|
| Researcher control | AI-generated survey material remained revisable, keeping the researcher responsible for the questionnaire rather than making generation an automatic commitment. |
| Product clarity | The product began with research intent before generating questions, giving the AI feature a clearer contract with the person using it. |
| MVP definition | The interaction direction established where AI could accelerate survey creation and where human judgement still had to remain explicit. |
